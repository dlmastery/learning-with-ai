The company

The unit of instruction is an accounting artifact.

The credit hour began as an accounting unit for a pension fund, generalised into a unit of knowledge because no other unit was available. Nobody at the time claimed fifteen weeks was a natural quantum of understanding — only that it was an auditable quantum of instruction delivered.

We are building the replacement unit: attention that is measured, verified, and spent where it changes something. Every figure in this deck traces to a labelled source, and every number we have had to withdraw is in a published corrections ledger.

What actually decides it

Executive function is a resource every product requires and none supplies.

Starting. Sustaining. Noticing you are stuck. Switching strategy. Every learning product assumes the learner brings these, and the ones who most need the product bring them least.

And the measured failure point is recognising the need — not accessing the help. Repairing recognition alone does not repair learning; the help has to arrive unprompted.

Two more results that redirect every roadmap here. "Make it engaging" does not work by making the learner feel good — two independent meta-analyses put learning at 0.27–0.39 and liking at 0.09–0.11 in the same studies. And a question holds attention because it can be attempted; an exhortation cannot — g = 0.54 on its own answer, g = 0.04 in general.

Independent confirmation

The best-instrumented tutor in the world failed for exactly this reason.

"It did not change student learning as much as many of us hoped it would."

Published against interest by an organisation with more instrumented learner-hours than almost anyone. And the diagnosis was not pedagogical: the mechanism worked when it fired, and it only fired when a student recognised they needed help and went to get it.

Invocation is part of the intervention. A tutor that must be summoned reaches the students who need it least.

So the system must fire unprompted

Which means it has to know the learner is stuck. Which nothing currently does.

The bottleneck, named

Pedagogy has no pytest.

chance

Across 223 real tutoring domains, no model beat chance at labelling an incorrect student action.

Looking at what a learner did and saying what is wrong with it — the operation that decides whether help can arrive unprompted — is currently unverifiable by the systems being sold to do it. Every other capability in this category is downstream of that one instrument.

The size of the prize

Learning is counted in opportunities, not days.

People barely differ in how fast they learn. They differ enormously in where they begin — and time-based models of learning fit poorly. 10–40× on elapsed calendar, 3–5× on engaged effort, one documented case near 300×.

A week's understanding in an hour. A year's retention in six hours spread over two months — because durability compresses 1×, and so does procedural skill.

Why humans stay in the loop

The tutor is not the cost line. The tutor is the labelling function.

What the trial measured

74.4% of 3,617 AI drafts accepted unedited

44.3% of tutor edits were slowing the model's questioning down

What that describes

The human was not writing content.

The human was acting as the verifier — in the exact domain where no automated check exists.

Every accept, edit and rejection is a labelled judgement about whether a pedagogical action was correct. The corpus that builds the missing instrument is a by-product of trading.

Stated precisely: the acceptance rate is measured. The authors say their design "precludes a rigorous measurement of throughput or efficiency." We treat 74.4% as a signal stream, never as a productivity claim.

Where the loop closes

In high-stakes exams, somebody else administers the truth.

SAT · JEE · NEET · GATE · gaokao. Proctored. Novel items each cycle. Months after study. Scored and reported — because the candidate wants it reported.

The delayed, unassisted, novel-item outcome costs every competitor a research programme. Here it is the customer's own goal, on a known date. The syllabus is closed, so prior knowledge is measurable in 15–40 seconds. And the exam supplies motivation, so we need no points economy — the mechanism that measurably undermines intrinsic motivation.

The economics, corrected

"Our costs fall as models get cheaper" is worth 0.43%.

0.43%

Inference as a share of the delivered cost of a human-supervised session. Two independent measurements converge.

Falling token prices are economically irrelevant to this P&L. The entire margin question is the leverage ratio — learner-hours supervised per paid tutor-hour — which we will report monthly from month one, because it is the only number that distinguishes a software business from a staffing business here.

What would disqualify us

Ask these of us, and of everyone else.

  1. Show me the delayed, unassisted, novel-item result — with n, and who held the item bank. One deployment moved its exit ticket +4pp and was null on the state test. That dissociation is the most reproducible finding in this literature.
  2. Have you ever run an arm against plain ChatGPT? A controlled trial (n = 371) found scaffolded generative AI no better on domain knowledge. If the pedagogy has never beaten the free substitute, the moat is a prompt.
  3. Learner-hours delivered ÷ paid tutor-hours, monthly, for 24 months. Flat while headcount grows means the AI is decorative and you are underwriting a staffing business at a software multiple.
Risks we are not hiding

Three things that could kill this.

1 · A frontier release solves error diagnosis

If a general model crosses from chance to competent at step-level diagnosis, the verifier becomes an API call. Largest risk, and not remote.

2 · The only audited comparable went the wrong way

A listed tutoring company rebuilt on "entirely new, AI-native codebases." Gross margin 67.5% → 58.0%; expert costs up $5.2M on revenue down $11.2M. Its 10-K: "There can be no assurance that our investments in AI will be beneficial to our business."

3 · One mentor across every learner is an unstudied risk we named ourselves

Thirty teachers make thirty different mistakes and the system averages them out. One system's errors are systematic and identical for every learner at once. There is no monitoring for it and no name for it in the literature — we call it correlated pedagogical error, and the mitigation is the same instrument as the moat.
