The mission

Everyone becomes a polymath.

A university in your pocket. Anything you want to understand, understood, at whatever depth you choose, starting this afternoon.

A tutor of the kind that costs $500 an hour, running for the price of the tokens it takes. For a nine-year-old, a surgeon, and a forty-year-old who always wondered how bridges stay up. The end of having to pick which curiosities you can afford.

The problem, in one person

An eleven-year-old can explain photosynthesis and cannot pass a worksheet about it.

She is served under a SELPA plan. She reads, and decoding costs her so much that by the end of a sentence the beginning is gone.

She is running a working memory that spends everything on the part everyone else automated years ago. About 15% of American school-age children are served under an individualised programme like hers. A census of ERIC and Europe PMC found 30 randomised trials of generative-AI tutoring involving students, and none that mention a disability, an IEP or special education. Every published effect in this field was measured on some other child.

Build it so it works for her and it works for everyone. Build it for everyone and it will fail her. She is the specification.

Why that is reachable

Polymathy was never a question of intelligence. It was a question of who could afford to be a beginner.

Koedinger et al., PNAS 2023, 1.3 million observations across 27 datasets. People barely differ in how fast they learn. They differ enormously in where they begin, and a time-based model of learning fits the data poorly.

Scientists work in three or four fields in a career. The fifth was not too hard. Entering it cost months of search, orientation, and not knowing which result mattered, with nobody to ask. That cost is attention, and attention just stopped being scarce.

Why now

The interfaces of a private tutor became software primitives.

See, hear, point and interrupt

Frontier live APIs now accept continuous speech, images and video, return native speech, call tools, support barge-in and span sessions.

Capability, documented in current APIs. The learning effect depends on what the system does with the shared work.

Compile an experience in the turn

Models generate working mini-apps, notebooks, animations, slides, audio, video and navigable worlds. The latency from confusion to manipulable object collapsed.

Capability. Every renderer still needs semantic, accessibility and outcome checks.

Remember and act between sessions

Agent runtimes persist state, execute tools, schedule jobs and hand work between specialists. One action can serve the learner UI, agents and human coordinators.

Architecture. The compounding asset is the learner-state-to-next-action map, not the replaceable base model.

What an hour buys

Almost none of a week is spent learning. That is the headroom.

A calendar week of a university course is 9 nominal student hours inside 112 waking ones. Go inside the 9 and the Beginning Teacher Evaluation Study traces allocated time to engaged time to time at a workable success rate: the same nominal day yields about 4 minutes of productive learning, or about 52. A 13× spread inside identical calendars.

Our estimate of the compression inference

10–40× on elapsed calendar. 3–5× on engaged effort.

Composed from measured parameters, and no trial of anyone becoming a polymath has been run. It is eminently runnable, and we intend to run it.

The idea that makes it work

Stop asking a machine to judge. Ask which belief predicted the answer.

Across 223 tutoring domains, the four models tested in an initial evaluation did not beat chance at labelling an incorrect student action. Scope that correctly, because it sharpens the claim: step-checking a model's own reasoning trace is well above chance, and ProcessBench reports open models competitive with GPT-4o at finding the earliest erroneous step. Reading a person is the open part.

We enumerate what a learner might believe. The live question becomes which of those predicted what we just saw, and that is an argmax over precomputed distributions.

Why it compounds

Every learner who is wrong in a new way makes the belief library better.

A stronger base model raises what the library is worth. It is the one row on our moat table that a frontier release makes more valuable.

Why a decade of money produced so little

The thing a learner can feel runs opposite to the thing that happened.

Bastani et al., PNAS 2025, gave about a thousand Turkish high-school students GPT-4 for maths practice. Practice scores rose 48%. On the unassisted exam afterwards the unguarded arm came out 17% worse than students who never had it.

The guarded arm landed at −0.004 and was unharmed, which is the finding that matters: guardrails have been measured to remove that harm and never to add benefit. It is one trial, and none of its 109 citing works has yet replicated it with a withdrawal design. Two smaller results agree: Buljan's three randomised comparisons of explanation formats (n = 334, immediate quiz) moved preference d ≈ 0.48 while knowledge moved zero, and Deslauriers found students taught more by active learning reporting they had learned less.

So satisfaction, engagement, streaks and time-on-app all proxy felt learning, and chasing them feels like winning throughout. An agent that teaches nothing can max out every one of them.

Competition

The incumbent sells a number it has never measured.

Powers and Rock ran seven estimators against about 6,700 SAT registrants: roughly one additional question correct per eight hours of coaching, or 6–8 scale points on verbal and 13–18 on mathematics. The two largest coaching firms advertised 120 and 140 combined points, documented by surveying previous customers. In India there is no causal estimate at all of what coaching does to a NEET or JEE rank.

A feature grid separates nobody. These three questions do, and we ask them of ourselves first:

  1. Learner-hours delivered ÷ paid tutor-hours, monthly, for 24 months. Flat while headcount grows means the buyer is underwriting a staffing business at a software multiple.
  2. Your delayed, unassisted, novel-item result, with n, and who held the item bank. One deployment moved its exit ticket +4 pp and came back null on the state test.
  3. Have you ever run an arm against plain ChatGPT? A trial at n = 371 found scaffolded generative AI no better than it. If the pedagogy has never beaten the free substitute, the moat is a prompt.
Where it starts

Where the world already grades the answer.

SAT · PSAT · NEET · JEE · GATE · EAMCET. Proctored, months later, on items nobody has practised, and the score comes back because the candidate wants it more than we do.

Everyone else funds a research programme to discover whether their product works. Here the truth signal is the customer's own objective: closed syllabus, published answer keys, decades of item-level response data, and hundreds of millions of candidates whose alternative is a room with four hundred other people in it.

Two things we will not claim

The gaokao is a policy risk before it is a market. The 2021 double-reduction order stopped new approvals for core-subject tutoring; one listed operator's revenue fell 62% in a year.

No top-down market size. Six of six analyst houses failed traceability when we checked, so we size this per candidate, bottom-up.

Go to market, ordered by friction

Authors and creators: live in days. Highest content velocity, no procurement.

Online course sites: weeks to months. Telemetry and a practice loop already running; they measure completion and nobody measures diagnosis.

Publishers: quarters. The corpus built with authors is why they sign.

Business model

Inference is 0.43% of what it costs to deliver a supervised hour.

Two measurements arrive at that figure independently. One published cost model prices the tokens inside a supervised session at £0.0037 against £0.861 delivered. Separately, a deployed programme's own API bill came to $1,419.66 across 429 tutors in two months, or $19.86 a year each, beside 200 hours of tutor time at a $23.10 mean wage.

So the margin question lands somewhere else entirely: the leverage ratio, learner-hours supervised per paid tutor-hour. We publish it monthly from month one. The one audited comparable that rebuilt on AI-native codebases went from 67.5% to 58.0% gross margin, so this gets demonstrated and never assumed.

What we sell, and what we buy

Direct to candidates working toward a dated exam, where the outcome arrives free.

To publishers, authors and course owners: the same book, with the practice loop instrumented and the diagnosis attached, as an addendum and never a migration.

The asset there is calibration. Generation drove item-production cost to zero and left calibration untouched, and automatic item generation breaks even at 173–247 items per fine-grained content area. Decades of response data is the calibration corpus a verifier needs, and no single publisher sees enough of a concept to build the layer alone.

Original measurements, including the ones that hurt

Our own permutation-based verification protocol, benchmarked over 768 generations on two models against a deterministic comparator: 87.5% recall and 87.5% false alarm, exactly at chance. Plain self-consistency beat it by 18.8 and 43.8 points. Published.

An abstention fix that took silent passes on wrong claims from 1 to 0 at no cost, and a sampling-budget null where recall stayed flat at 99.1% while p95 latency rose 6.8×.

Everything above is public: the demos, the harnesses, the raw result files, and the ledger of what we got wrong.

What it costs to be wrong

What kills this, in the order we worry about it.

1 · The models get good at diagnosis

Our structure collapses into an API call. The largest risk, and not remote. The hedge is that the belief library is data we own and a stronger model reads it better.

2 · One mind, ten million children

Thirty teachers make thirty different mistakes and they cancel. One system's mistakes are identical for everyone at once. The mitigation is the same instrument as the moat.

3 · It works, and it widens the gap

Untargeted delivery reliably helps the already-strong more: the best-designed frontier trial to date loaded at +0.195 SD per SD of baseline attainment. Across eight targeted interventions, none widened gaps and several narrowed them sharply, so this is a design failure and never a tax. We report gap change as a primary outcome. If it widens gaps it has failed, whatever the average says.

The vision

Nobody's thumb, ever again.

Ekalavya was refused a teacher, taught himself, became the better archer, and they took his thumb for it.

That is the history of education in one story: never a shortage of talent, always a shortage of access, and then a system defending itself when someone routed around it. The tutor was always the intervention that worked. It was never the one that could be given to everyone.

That constraint is gone, and nothing has been rebuilt around its absence. In five years a child anywhere holds the faculty of a university that knows only her, and the word polymath stops describing a rare person and starts describing an ordinary one.
