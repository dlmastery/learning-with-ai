Demo 17 · The mechanism

The widget comes before the sentence

Read the raw HTML of Ciechanowski's Gears and an ordering falls out that is invisible when you read the page normally: the manipulable figure is emitted before the prose that resolves it, 30 times in one article and 120 times in Moon — and the reader is never once asked to pause. Four figures below do the same thing to one concept.

Every number and crossover computed live Gates enforced, not suggested The resolving prose is written, not generated
What this is

The one technique in the whole inventory that nobody has ported into an AI system, built as a working sequence. Four figures on exponential growth. One new degree of freedom each. A gate on every one that will not open until you have moved something.

Why it matters

Sanderson says the prediction is where the learning happens and concedes viewers are “a little bit more passive in that moment.” A pause is a request, and requests are declined. Ciechanowski deleted the thing that would have to be paused — an article has no clock, so the widget simply sits there, unresolved, and you move it because there is nothing else to do.

How to use it

You will not be able to read the explanations until you have moved the figures. That is the whole design, enforced in code rather than asked for in a sentence. When all four are open, the switch at the bottom re-orders the page so the prose comes first. The difference is the thing this page is about.

The parameter values are not neutral. Prediction has no main effect. The entire measured effect is carried by the expectancy-violation interaction, p = .002. Predicting and being right does approximately nothing. So every figure below is set at values where the common wrong intuition and the truth give visibly different answers. A prediction step that mostly confirms is a cost with no benefit.

Four figures, one concept

Exponential growth. Each figure adds exactly one control to the one before it, and none of them explains anything until you have used it.

The A/B, felt rather than argued

Flip the ordering. The same four figures, the same four paragraphs, the same numbers, with the prose first and the gates removed. Nothing is withheld and nothing is required.

The spec, in one sentence

What a generator would have to emit

For every load-bearing claim, emit a manipulable figure that instantiates it, render it before the prose that resolves it, introduce one new degree of freedom per figure, and do not advance until the learner has moved something.

Four constraints, each doing work. Instantiates rather than illustrates. The figure has to be the claim, not a picture of it. The order is non-negotiable: after the prose, it is a demonstration rather than a prediction. One degree of freedom keeps the search space small enough to reason about. And the gate makes the prediction compulsory without ever asking.

What this proves / what it does not

It proves the ordering is mechanisable. That is a checkable property rather than a style note. Every figure above is emitted before its paragraph; every gate is a real predicate over a real input event; every number in every reveal is computed from the control you moved, including the crossover in Figure 4, which is found by search rather than looked up. A generator could be required to satisfy these four constraints and be rejected when it does not, exactly as a simulation is rejected for a null slider.

It also shows the technique is cheap. Four figures, no library, no build step, no model. The reason nobody has ported it is not cost.

Three conditions of use, in the order they will bite you:

  1. The commitment step is an addition, not Ciechanowski's. His pages have no multiple-choice; the widget alone is the gate. It is added here because the measured warrant is the violation, and a violation needs something to violate. Whether a one-click commitment is enough to carry the interaction has not been tested.
  2. A gate is a lock, and locks have a cost. A learner who cannot proceed is a learner who can leave. Ciechanowski's version has no lock at all — the figure is simply the only thing on the screen. That is a stronger design than this one, and it requires the article to have nothing else in it.
  3. None of this is evidence of learning. The p = .002 interaction is evidence about prediction-and-violation in a controlled setting, not about this page, this concept, or this implementation. What is demonstrated here is that the ordering is buildable and enforceable — which is a claim about engineering, and the claim the field has been failing at.
