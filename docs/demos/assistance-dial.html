Demo 16 · Mechanisms

The support that helps you is harming them

Fifty years of aptitude–treatment research produced exactly one interaction that replicated. It is not a style, a modality or a personality type. It is how much help to give, on this topic, right now — worth d = +0.505 to a novice and −0.428 to an expert, which means the same support is a gift and a tax depending on who receives it.

Surface computed from the two marginals Six probe items, authored Does not extend to multimedia design (p = 0.14)
What this is

A working assistance dial. Four levels of support — fully worked, two-step completion, one-step completion, independent — plotted against a measured estimate of what you know about one topic. The surface is not drawn by hand: every cell is assistance × d(knowledge), interpolated between the two published marginals.

Why it matters

Almost every tutoring system stores support level as a learner trait. The interaction is not a trait — it is per topic and it changes sign. A learner who is an expert in chapter 4 and a novice in chapter 5 needs opposite treatment within the same hour, and a system that got that backwards is not merely unhelpful. At −0.428 it is subtracting.

How to use it

Three items, about twenty seconds, and you are placed on the axis. Do the second topic as well — the same person usually lands somewhere else, which is the entire per-topic argument in one screen. The preference slider further down is live, real, and connected to nothing. That gap is the demonstration.

1 · Entry has to be measured, not asked

Stated preference is not a bad proxy for prior knowledge. It is an unrelated quantity: learners' preference for the fluent condition moves at d ≈ 0.48 while their knowledge moves zero. So the dial cannot be set by asking. Three items, weighted by difficulty, scored here in the page.

Topic probe
Pick a topic. The estimate is Σ(wᵢ·correctᵢ) / Σwᵢ mapped onto [0.05, 0.95], where w is the item's difficulty weight. The rule is printed so you can disagree with it.
That is the whole argument for per-topic.

2 · The surface

Every cell below is a · d(r), where a is the assistance fraction (1.00 fully worked → 0.00 independent) and d(r) = 0.505 − 0.933·r is the linear interpolation between the two measured marginals. The value is a predicted difference from independent practice at the same knowledge level, in standard deviations. Blue is help. Orange is harm.

3 · The slider that changes nothing

This one is wired to the recommendation the way stated preference is wired to learning: it is recorded, it is honest, and it has no path into the computation.

The asymmetry is the design rule. Under-assisting a novice costs a gain you could have had. Over-assisting an expert costs them something they already had — it is an active harm, not a missed opportunity. When the estimate is uncertain, the cheap error and the expensive error are not the same error, and the default should sit on the cheap side of the crossover rather than in the middle of it.

4 · What this proves / what it does not

What this page shows working
  • That the crossover is forced by the two published numbers, not added by us. Any monotone interpolation between +0.505 at the novice end and −0.428 at the expert end has to cross zero, and it crosses at 0.505 / 0.933 = 0.541. The surface is that arithmetic and nothing else.
  • That entry can be measured in under a minute. Three weighted items place a reader on the axis well enough to pick between four assistance levels — and the page times your run, so the claim is checkable on your own clock rather than asserted.
  • That per-topic is not a slogan. Take both probes. If your two estimates land on opposite sides of the crossover line, a system storing one support level against your name is guaranteed to be wrong for half your session, and the page will say so with your own numbers in it.
What it does not
  • Three items is a placement, not a measurement. A six-item bank cannot separate 0.55 from 0.62, and near the crossover that difference decides the treatment. The honest use is to pick a starting level and re-place after the first block, not to trust the third decimal.
  • The surface is linear between two endpoints. The underlying meta reports only the two marginals; the shape in between is unmeasured. A sigmoid through the same endpoints would move the crossover somewhat and change none of the conclusions, which is exactly why the page plots sign rather than magnitude.
  • The heterogeneity is enormous. I² ≈ 88–91% across 60 studies, 176 effects, N = 5,924. This is a real law with noisy boundaries. Treat the crossover as a region you should probe in, not a fence you can stand on.
  • It does not generalise to instructional design as a whole. Noetel's meta-meta-analysis found prior knowledge did not consistently moderate multimedia design effects, p = 0.14. So the survivor is narrower than "personalise instruction to prior knowledge". It is prior knowledge moderates how much help to give — assistance, guidance, worked examples, scaffolding.
