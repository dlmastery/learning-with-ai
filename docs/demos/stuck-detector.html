Demo 19 · The mechanism

Help that arrives before you ask

The measured failure point is not reaching the help. It is recognising you need it — and the best-instrumented tutor in the world published a null traced to exactly that. This page builds the detector that removes the summoning step, using only signals a learner emits by acting, and then refuses to pretend the threshold is easy.

Detector, sweep and optimum all computed Deterministic seed — the same session every time The learner is simulated No affect inference, by law and by design
What this is

A running estimate of stuck-ness over one simulated session, computed from five behavioural signals and an intervention threshold you control. Underneath it, a ground-truth label the detector never sees: at each moment the learner is in flow, in productive struggle, or wheel-spinning.

Why it matters

Cognitive Tutor logs, twenty-five years ago: after three consecutive errors a hint was requested only 34% of the time, and 68% of hint levels were viewed for under one second. The help was there. It was not summoned, and when summoned it was not read. Khanmigo's published null is the same diagnosis, a quarter of a century later.

How to use it

There is one control that matters and it has no right setting. Slide the threshold down and the detector rescues wheel-spinners while interrupting people who were about to get there on their own; slide it up and the reverse. §5 puts the same threshold in front of two learners and shows it cannot be one number.

The tension this page must not dodge. Productive failure measures g = 0.36–0.58, and the finding is blunt: adding help to the struggle does not help. A system optimised to notice you are stuck and intervene is optimised to destroy the mechanism that produces that effect. The resolution proposed here — intervene on the wrong kind of stuck — is a design claim, not a measured one, and the cost of a false interrupt on this page is set accordingly high.

2 · What the detector is allowed to see

Five signals, each one a by-product of the learner acting on the problem. Nothing is inferred about how they feel, and nothing is observed that they did not choose to emit.

SignalStatusWeightWhat it is
response_latencyused0.90 Seconds since the last action, standardised. Long latency alone means nothing — it is what productive struggle looks like too.
edit_backtrackused0.35 Deletions and reversals in the window. A learner rewriting is a learner still trying.
repeat_error_runused0.62 Consecutive identical error signatures. The load-bearing signal. Repetition without variation is the definition of wheel-spinning.
no_state_changeused0.22 Windows since the answer last changed at all, capped at ten. Distinguishes thinking from stalling.
help_avoidanceused0.80 Help offered and declined, or a hint opened and closed inside a second — the behaviour that produced the 68% figure.
affect / frustrationrefused— Emotion inference in education is prohibited outright under EU AI Act Art. 5(1)(f) — a prohibition, not a risk tier. Not a compliance cost: nothing on this page needs it.
gaze / webcamrefused— Continuous biometric capture of a child, for a signal the behavioural set already carries.
keystroke dynamics as mood proxyrefused— Emotion inference wearing a behavioural costume. The prohibition is on the inference, not the sensor.

4 · Against waiting to be asked

The comparison that justifies the whole apparatus. The alternative to a detector is not nothing — it is a help button, which is what every system already ships.

—
The 34% is measured; its application here is arithmetic. The rate comes from Cognitive Tutor logs — after three consecutive errors a hint followed 34% of the time. Applying it to this simulated session tells you what a help button would have caught if these learners behaved like those learners. It is an illustration of a measured rate, not a measurement of this page.

5 · Why the threshold cannot be one number

The same detector, the same threshold, two learners. Students in the bottom quintile of prerequisite knowledge wheel-spin 50% of the time against 10% for the top quintile — half of a weak learner's session spent going nowhere, for a reason that was fixed before the session started.

What this proves / what it does not

Read this as a list of who pays when each part is wrong.

ComponentStatus on this pageWho pays if it is wrong
The detector Real. A logistic sum over five declared features with published weights, smoothed, run on every window. You can read every coefficient in §2. Nobody yet — the weights are a design proposal and no trial has fitted them. In production, a badly fitted weight on repeat_error_run is the difference between the two failure modes.
The trade-off Real, and the shape is the finding: swept exhaustively, both curves counted at every threshold. There is no setting where both are zero. The learner, either way. This is the honest structure of the problem and it does not go away with a better model.
The learner Simulated, from a seeded generator. The 50% / 10% wheel-spin rates are measured; everything about how those states look in the signal stream is assumed. This page. If real productive struggle and real wheel-spinning are less separable than the generator makes them, every number in §3 is optimistic.
The latency-only comparison Honest but circular. It fails here because the generator gives productive struggle the longer latencies — which is what the observational literature reports, and which this page assumed rather than showed. Anyone who reads it as evidence. It is a demonstration that a single-signal detector can invert, not proof that it does.
The premise — that arriving unasked helps Not shown, and there is a null against the adjacent claim. Carnegie Mellon built help-seeking support; help-seeking improved durably, months after the support was removed. Domain learning did not move. Everyone, including this design. The honest reading is that help-seeking was a necessary condition that turned out not to be sufficient. Supplying it removes a blocker; it does not teach.
What survives that null. Invocation is part of the intervention — a tutor that must be summoned reaches the students who need it least, and that much is measured twice, in two decades, by two organisations. What is not established is that removing the summoning step produces learning rather than merely producing more help. Any design that supplies executive function externally has to explain why it would not land in the same place, and this one does not yet have that explanation.
