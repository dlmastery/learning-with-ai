The Protégé That Stays Wrong

You teach it. It adopts your explanation including your errors, applies them visibly to fresh problems, and does not quietly self-correct. The gap between what you meant and what you said is the entire pedagogical payload.

Computed live g = −0.02 without expectancy Mechanism untested
What this is

A protégé you teach. You write a rule in your own words; a parser applies that rule literally to eight fresh fraction problems and shows its working. It has no knowledge of fractions and no way to quietly fix a bad rule.

Why it matters

Learning by teaching is g = 0.48 when the expectancy to teach is set before study, and g = −0.02 without it. Every commercial model silently repairs a learner's wrong rule, which destroys the mechanism. The gap between what you meant and what you actually said is the entire pedagogical payload.

How to use it

Accept the expectancy first — that ordering is the effect. Then type a rule, even a wrong one (try add tops, add bottoms), and press run. Watch which item it happens to pass. Edit the rule and re-run until the suite is green.

COMPUTED. Everything below runs in your browser. The rule you type is parsed by a real recursive-descent expression parser (~60 lines, no library), evaluated on every test item, and compared against exact rational arithmetic. Nothing here is a replay, and nothing is a language model. The protégé is literal on purpose: it has no capacity to notice that your rule is wrong.

1 · The expectancy, set before you study anything

This ordering is not decoration. Kobayashi's 2024 meta-analysis (k = 39) found that teaching after studying with a teaching expectancy is g = 0.48 [0.34, 0.63], while teaching after studying without one is g = −0.02 [−0.14, 0.11] — indistinguishable from zero. The obligation has to be announced first. So:

Your obligation
Read this before you look at anything else on the page.

You are about to teach a protégé how to add fractions. It knows nothing. It will believe exactly what you write, and only what you write. It will then sit a test in front of you, and you will see its score. It will not correct your rule, cover for you, or infer what you meant.

2 · Teach the rule

The protégé works on problems of the form a/b + c/d. Say what its numerator and denominator should be. You may write it in words — the phrase table below shows exactly what vocabulary the parser recognises — or edit the formulas directly.

Do not let the numbers migrate. g = 0.56 is human learning-by-teaching and g = 0.43 is peer tutoring’s tutor-side gain. Neither is a measurement of the artefact on this page. The honest statement is: the mechanism the strongest evidence points at has, as far as this survey could establish, never been built and tested in the form demonstrated here — a protégé that adopts a learner’s stated rule literally and refuses to repair it.

What this proves / what it does not

What it proves
  • A protégé that is faithful to a learner’s stated rule is buildable in a few dozen lines of deterministic code — no model, no network, no key.
  • Non-self-correction is implementable as an explicit property, not a hoped-for behaviour. The engine has no path by which it could notice the rule is wrong.
  • A test suite can make a misconception visible as a score, including the case where a bad rule accidentally passes an easy item — which is a real property of assessment design, not a bug in the demo.
  • The teaching expectancy can be set reliably, for free, before every study episode. That is the moderator carrying the effect, and software is unusually good at it.
What it does not prove
  • That it teaches anyone anything. No learning outcome is measured on this page, and none could be.
  • That the g = 0.48 expectancy effect transfers from human tutees to a symbolic engine. It might; nobody has run the trial.
  • That this generalises beyond a domain with exact, checkable ground truth. Fraction addition is the easy case. History, ethics and interpretation do not have an = operator.
  • That a learner who watches the protégé fail will revise their own rule rather than blame the protégé. That attribution is exactly the thing the trial would have to measure.
