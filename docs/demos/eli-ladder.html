Demo 09 · The mechanisms

Three Rungs, Not Five

One concept, entropy, written at three genuinely different altitudes. Not a staircase you climb from the bottom: a library you enter, with the door chosen by a short measured probe and never by what you say you want. The fidelity rule that keeps a simplification honest is enforced here in code, and you can watch it reject a simplification that sounds perfectly reasonable.

Computed live Fidelity rule enforced in code
What this is

One genuinely hard concept written at three distinct altitudes, presented as a library you enter rather than a staircase you climb, with the fidelity rule enforced in code.

Why it matters

Three rungs beat two (p = 0.032); five did not beat three (p = 0.738). So ELI10/15/20/25 is one level too many, and entry must be measured, because where the two have been put side by side they come apart: in Buljan's three randomised comparisons (n = 334 adults, an infographic against a plain-language Cochrane summary, immediate quiz), preference moved d ≈ 0.48 while knowledge moved zero. A simplification may drop precision. It may never falsify ontology.

How to use it

Take the short probe and let it choose your door rather than picking one. Then open the violation toggle: a plausible-sounding simplification that breaks ontology, and the reason a full semester of instruction does not shift that particular error.

Computed means: the probe below is really administered, the prerequisite closure is really evaluated, the entry rung is really derived from a stated rule, and the fidelity checker really runs field-by-field over structured claims and really rejects the violating rung. Nothing on this page is a replayed transcript.

Why three and not five. The obvious design is ELI10 / ELI15 / ELI20 / ELI25, four or five neat rungs. The one experiment that directly compared granularities found three-step beat two-step (p = 0.032) and five-step did not beat three-step (p = 0.738, Mdiff = 0.16, 95% CI [−0.78, 1.09]). The returns diminish, and they diminish early. Charts at the bottom.

Three items over entropy's prerequisite closure, plus a load rating. Two minutes.

Why preference is inadmissible as an input. Buljan et al. 2018 ran three randomised comparisons: n = 334 adults, an infographic against a plain-language Cochrane summary of the same review, scored on an immediate quiz. Preference between the two versions moved d ≈ 0.48 while knowledge moved zero, and the bias survives explicit debiasing. One format contrast in one adult population is not a law about learners. Worse, the direction is not random: a simplified account raises confidence and perceived credibility independently of competence, so a learner who has just read the easy rung is more confident and therefore less likely to ask for the harder one. Preference-driven laddering has a built-in downward ratchet. The dropdown above exists as an override and an audit trail. It may never be the default input.

3 · The fidelity rule, enforced

A rung may drop a great deal. There are exactly four things it may never falsify.

4 · Why a semester does not fix it

The reason ontology is on the "never" list rather than the "prefer not to" list is empirical. Chi's account distinguishes two kinds of wrong belief. A misconception that lies within the correct ontological category — a wrong number, a missing term, a confused sub-mechanism — is revisable by ordinary instruction. A misconception that places the concept in the wrong ontological category is not. Instruction does not replace it; it produces a synthetic model, a hybrid in which the student holds the taught account and the miscategorised account simultaneously and deploys whichever the question cues.

The measured instance. A study of university chemistry students tracked the classical/quantum hybrid population — students who hold "electrons orbit the nucleus like planets" alongside the quantum account — and found that population unchanged across a full semester of instruction. The Bohr-orbit error is the textbook case of an ontological miscategorisation: it puts a stationary state into the category "object on a trajectory."

Applying that to entropy is INFERENCE, not measurement. Nobody has run the semester-long study on "entropy is disorder" specifically. What is measured is that cross-category errors in general are robust, and that this particular cross-category error in chemistry survived a semester. The claim being made here is that "entropy is a measure of messiness" is the same class of error — it relocates an emergent statistical property of a distribution into the category of a visible property of an arrangement — and therefore carries the same prognosis.

This is the whole argument for enforcing the rule at authoring time rather than fixing it at teaching time. A precision error costs a correction. An ontology error costs a re-categorisation, and the field's evidence says re-categorisation mostly does not happen.

5 · The evidence

What this proves / what it does not

What this page proves
  • That the fidelity rule is mechanisable. It is not a stylistic aspiration. Represent a rung as structured claims rather than prose and the four never-falsify invariants become a forty-line pure function that returns pass or fail with a named failure class.
  • That entry-by-probe is buildable, cheap, and produces a different answer from entry-by-preference, which you can watch happen when your stated preference and your computed rung disagree.
  • That three genuinely distinct altitudes of the same concept can be written such that every proposition at rung 1 survives verbatim into rung 3 — nothing is retracted, only added. That is a strong constraint and it is satisfiable.
What this page does not prove
  • That the fidelity rule improves learning. It has never been tested. The rule is assembled from Chi's ontological account plus the entailment requirement; the assembly itself is INFERENCE. No trial has compared ladders written under this rule against ladders written without it.
  • That the checker is complete. It enforces four invariants over hand-authored fields. It cannot tell you whether the prose actually says what the fields claim — that gap is real, and closing it needs an entailment check between rung n and rung n+1, which is a different and harder problem.
  • That the probe is validated. Three items is far below what any measurement standard would accept for a placement decision. The rule it feeds is the published one; the instrument is a demonstration.
  • That three is the right number of rungs for your concept. The p = 0.032 / p = 0.738 result is 166 nine-year-olds learning network routing. It is the only direct granularity comparison anyone has run, which says more about how thin this literature is than about how strong the finding is.
