The referral, not the confidence

A concern surfaces mid-session. What happens next is the highest-stakes path in any learning system, and it is almost entirely a routing problem: the duty is immediate and personal, it goes around the parent, and the usual person being unavailable is not a reason to delay. Below, the path runs. Then the measurement that decides whether any of it worked — and it is not how ready anyone feels.

What this is

A safeguarding decision path with the routing made explicit — recipient, timing, fallback, record — plus the triage arithmetic underneath it and the two dashboards that the same term of data can produce.

Why it matters

Recognition is not the bottleneck. NIS-4 found that at least 80% of school-recognised maltreatment never reached investigation, while child protective services would have investigated 72% of it. Schools were seeing it. The referral was not being made. Every safeguarding feature is aimed at the wrong half of that sentence.

How to use it

Do not start with the routing. Start at section 4, move the training slider, and watch a dashboard go entirely green while the only number that matters does not move at all. Then come back and read what the path is supposed to produce.

2 · The path, executed

Six layers. The design principle underneath all of them is one line: detect broadly, act conservatively, route to a human always, resolve automatically never.

3 · Detect broadly — and what that costs

Triage runs on every learner turn and is tuned for recall, not precision, because the dominant failure of language models in crisis handling is omission: a measured 13.2% omission rate, concentrated in crisis and suicidal-ideation prompts. A false positive costs a human five minutes. A false negative costs a child. Those are not commensurable, so this page will not combine them into one score.

A term of sessions, at a chosen threshold
2,000 sessions a week. Signals are drawn from two normal distributions separated by d′ = 3.0, which is roughly the separation implied by the F1 ≈ 0.88–0.91 reported for ideation detection and risk assessment on real hotline transcripts.
Watch the two F1 tiles. The same detector, at the same threshold, scores near the published 0.88–0.91 on a corpus where half the cases are positive and falls through the floor at the prevalence a real deployment sees. Nothing about the detector changed; the base rate did. That is why F1 ≈ 0.88 is adequate for routing and inadequate for deciding, and why the human recipient in Layer 4 is not optional.

And the benchmark cuts both ways. Clinician review of more than 20,000 real conversations with a purpose-built system found zero cases of suicide risk that failed to receive crisis resources, while adversarial benchmarks predicted far worse. Benchmarks are not deployment evidence in either direction. Ecological audit is.

4 · The same term, two dashboards

A randomised trial of gatekeeper training moved confidence by ES 1.22 and identification behaviour by nothing. Both dashboards below are computed from one simulated cohort of school staff under the training dose you set. Neither is fabricated to make a point; the left one is what happens when you measure the thing that moves.

Large movement in how prepared people feel; zero movement in what they do. That pattern is the felt-learning trap wearing a safeguarding costume, and it is the reason a safeguarding feature must be measured on referrals made — never on staff confidence, never on completion of a module, never on a self-reported readiness score. If your dashboard would look identical whether or not a single additional child was referred, it is not a safeguarding dashboard.

7 · What this proves, and what it must never be mistaken for

What the page establishes
  • The routing is fully specifiable, and specifying it is most of the work. Recipient, timing, fallback, record contents, the four things the turn must do and the five it must not — none of that needs a model. It needs a decision that someone wrote down before a child said anything.
  • Unavailability is designed out rather than apologised for. Turn off the named recipient and the page does not degrade gracefully; it refuses to ship. Turn off the fallback and the chain stalls visibly. That is the correct behaviour: guidance is explicit that the lead being unavailable "should not delay appropriate action being taken."
  • Recall-tuning has an arithmetic price, and the page names it in hours. The triage panel converts a threshold into children missed per week and reviewer-hours per week, in the two units the decision is actually made in. It also shows what happens to a published F1 when it meets a real base rate, which settles an argument usually conducted on vibes.
  • A confidence dashboard and a referral dashboard can be built from the same term. One goes green at every dose. The other does not move at all. If a product team is allowed to choose which to display, the choice has already been made for them by which one is easier to move.
What it must never be mistaken for
  • This is not a safeguarding policy and cannot be used as one. It is a routing demonstration built from published statutory guidance for schools in one jurisdiction. Duties, timeframes and who counts as a mandated reporter vary by jurisdiction, and the survey this comes from explicitly declines to assert the US position because it could not verify it against a primary source. Get counsel. Do not get it from a demo page.
  • The cohort in section 4 is simulated, and only the effect sizes are real. The confidence arm is drawn to reproduce the trial's measured ES 1.22 and the behaviour arm to reproduce its null. What the simulation shows is the shape of the reporting problem — that two honest dashboards can disagree completely — not a new measurement of anything.
  • Detection quality is a routing input, never a decision. F1 ≈ 0.88 means roughly one in eight is wrong, and the page deliberately offers no way to let the system act on its own confidence. Every escalation terminates in a human decision that is recorded, and there is no auto-resolve anywhere in the path.
  • And the metric discipline is the part to carry away. Track omission rate as the primary safety indicator, disaggregated by language, dialect and reading level — guidance names language barriers specifically as a reason children fail to disclose, and a triage classifier trained on fluent standard-English disclosure will under-detect exactly those children. Referrals made is the outcome. Confidence and completion are not proxies for it; they are the two numbers that have been measured moving while it stayed still.
