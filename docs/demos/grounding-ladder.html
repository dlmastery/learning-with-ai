Six Rungs of Proof

A model asserts a formula. Before a learner sees it, something has to check it. Three of those checks run below, on your machine, right now, timed with performance.now(), against a corpus of textbook formulas and five classes of deliberate corruption.

Computed live Timed on your hardware k = 8 buys nothing over k = 1
What this is

A real verification stack running in your browser. Twenty textbook formulas, five ways of breaking each, and three checkers (dimensional, numeric, symbolic) timed with performance.now(). Nothing here is hardcoded.

Why it matters

If a tutor can be checked, it can contradict a confident learner without anyone asserting authority. The measured surprise: numeric checking is the cheap default at near-perfect recall, while symbolic escalation costs far more for almost nothing. That is the opposite of how everyone builds it.

How to use it

Run the suite and read the per-rung table. Then raise k, the number of random substitutions, and watch recall stay flat while latency climbs. That is a null we measured. Look for the row where our own symbolic checker gets it wrong; it is disclosed, not hidden.

COMPUTED, and unusually literally. There is no recorded result anywhere on this page. A hand-written expression parser, a dimensional-analysis engine, a numeric sampler and a canonical-form comparator are all in the page source. They build the mutant corpus at load, run every check, and report the wall-clock cost measured on the device you are holding. Your numbers will differ from the corpus figures quoted at the bottom, and that is the point: those were measured on a different machine, in a different language, and are reported here for comparison, not as this page's output.

1 · The corpus

Textbook formulas with declared dimensions, plus five mutation classes applied to each: sign flip, coefficient change, swapped variable, dropped term, wrong exponent. Mutants are generated at load by a seeded generator, so the corpus is identical every time you reload. Every mutant is printed, so you can check the generator did what it says.

Some mutations are not errors. Swapping a and b in sqrt(a^2 + b^2) produces a different string and the same function. The builder detects these with a 64-sample equivalence probe and files them as null mutants: cases where the correct answer is pass. Together with a hand-written set of identity rewrites, they form the false-alarm denominator. A detector with no false-alarm denominator is not a detector, it is an alarm.

Reference-free. Needs only declared units and no correct answer to compare against, so it is the only rung that can check a formula nobody has seen before.

L2 · Numeric

Substitute random values into both the reference and the candidate, compare. Needs a reference. Cannot handle universals.

L3 · Structural
—
not run

Canonical-form comparison: flatten, fold, collect powers, sort, expand small integer powers, compare strings.

Times are per single check, obtained by running the whole corpus in a loop until at least 60 ms of wall clock has elapsed and then dividing. performance.now() is deliberately coarsened by browsers, and a single check falls far below its resolution. Expect the L3/L2 ratio here to be much larger than the corpus’s ~3×. That is not a contradiction: the corpus timed a Python harness where fixed per-call overhead dominated both rungs and compressed the ratio, while these two rungs are bare JavaScript and the difference between “evaluate a float expression once” and “rebuild a canonical form and compare strings” is exposed in full. The ordering is the finding; the ratio is hardware.

The hole is where the teaching is. Symbolic checking is complete in theory and 38.3% incomplete in the implementation everyone actually uses. The failures cluster in sums, definite integrals and transforms, which is precisely the material a physics or engineering learner is working through. Meanwhile the numeric rung is 3–5× cheaper, has no domain holes of that kind, and loses one item in 113. Buying the last 0.9% costs you a 38.3% blind spot you cannot see into. That trade is the whole design decision.
And the rule that governs L3’s output. By Richardson’s theorem, a simplifier returning “not zero” is not a proof of inequality. A sound symbolic rung emits PASS or ABSTAIN — never FAIL. The comparator on this page reports differs, which is a suspicion and is scored as a catch only because it is compared against a known-correct reference. Remove the reference and that column collapses.

What this proves / what it does not

What it proves
  • Formula verification is cheap: sub-millisecond, in a browser, in plain JavaScript, with no dependency. There is no cost argument for shipping unchecked formulas.
  • The rungs are genuinely different instruments: L1 is reference-free but blind to coefficients and signs; L2 needs a reference and catches nearly everything; L3 is exact and brittle. Ordering them cheapest-first is a measurable saving, not an aesthetic.
  • The k null replicates on your hardware inside the declared domain: recall flat, cost linear. Anyone who samples eight points because eight feels safer is paying for a feeling.
  • And the sharper version, which this page measures rather than asserts: k only helps when the sampling domain is wrong. Switch to the wide or signed domain and recall at k = 1 drops, then climbs back as k rises. Extra samples are a subsidy for a badly-declared domain, and declaring it correctly costs nothing.
  • Sampling outside the declared domain also produces false alarms, on expressions that are genuinely equal: √(a/b) and √a/√b separate the instant b goes negative. This reproduces the corpus’s ordering (0 narrow, 1 wide, 2 signed) from independent code.
What it does not prove
  • That a verified formula teaches anybody anything. Grounding removes a failure mode. It does not add instruction.
  • That this corpus is representative. Twenty clean algebraic formulas is the easy end. Nothing here has a definite integral, a transform, a limit, or a universally quantified claim — the exact places where the real corpus reports its holes.
  • That the mutations resemble how models actually fail. These are five mechanical corruptions. Real model errors are more often plausible substitutions from a neighbouring topic, which a numeric check catches just as easily but a human reader does not.
  • That L3 here is SymPy. It is a canonical-form comparator of about eighty lines. It handles commutativity, associativity, constant folding, power collection and small integer expansion. It does not do trigonometric identities, logarithm laws or partial fractions, and it tells you so in the false-alarm table by getting the log rule wrong in front of you.
