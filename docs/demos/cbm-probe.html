Demo 13 · The mechanisms

Measurement That Changes Something

Generate brief equivalent-form probes. Graph them against a goal line. Apply a stated decision rule. And then — the part that carried the entire achievement effect in the trial that established this — emit a prescribed instructional change from a known-good menu. A dashboard that does everything except the last step is the arm that moved nothing.

Computed live Probes generated and audited in the page Shows a null result

Computed means: the probe forms below are really generated item by item, their composition is really audited, the simulated learner really answers them type by type, the decision rule really evaluates, the error profile really drives the selection from the menu, and the growth-claim gate really refuses. The learner is simulated; everything done to the learner is real code.

1 · Generate the probes

CBM-Math computation probes, scored in digits correct. Two generators.

Generator
Both produce 25-item computation probes that look identical on the page. Only one of them controls what actually drives difficulty.
One form, as administered
The warning this section exists for. Six DIBELS oral-reading passages "developed to be comparable based on readability formulas" produced mean fluency ranging 67.9 to 93.9 WCPM — a 26-point spread, roughly a semester of apparent growth, generated entirely by which passage the child happened to read. It biased both the shape and the slope of the growth trajectories. Readability-based equivalence is invalid; explicit equating on response data is required.

And the audit above cannot fix that. Regrouping count and available digits are surface features. Controlling a surface feature is exactly what the readability formulas were doing when they failed. What this page can demonstrate is that a generator which does not control the difficulty-bearing feature produces forms that visibly are not equivalent. What it cannot demonstrate is that the controlled generator produces forms that are. Only response data from real students, and explicit equating on it, can establish that — and the generative analogue of "the prompt constrains difficulty" will fail the same way readability did.

2 · What non-equivalence does to the trend

Same learner, same growth, different probe sets
A simulated learner with identical true growth is administered 20 weeks of probes from each generator, 400 times over. The quantity of interest is how much the estimated slope wanders around the true one.

4 · The known-good menu

The component that carried the effect. It is a lookup table, and that is the point.

Why a menu and not a generator. The prescription has to come from a set of changes that have themselves been evaluated. A system that invents a novel instructional change on the fly and prescribes it has replaced the active ingredient — a principled change drawn from known-good options — with an unprincipled one, which is the condition that produced no gain. In special education specifically, the evidence base is unusually replication-rich, and the system's job there is fidelity and dosage of known-good intervention, not invention.

What this proves / What it does not

What this page proves
  • That the whole loop is buildable end to end — generate, audit, administer, graph, decide, prescribe — with every step stated as a rule rather than as a judgement.
  • That the difference between the two arms of the 1991 trial is one function call. Both modes above run the identical rule on the identical data. One of them returns an instruction and the other returns nothing, and that is the entire architectural content of the finding.
  • That non-equivalent forms measurably corrupt the slope estimate. The replication run computes the standard deviation of the estimated slope under each generator, and the surface-matched one is visibly worse — from a mechanism you can inspect, namely that the number of regrouping items varies from form to form and the learner is weak on regrouping.
  • That the gates can be enforced rather than recommended. The system refuses a growth claim below 14 points and says so, instead of drawing a trend line through four.
What this page does not prove
  • That these probes are equivalent. The audit controls surface features. Controlling surface features is precisely what failed for readability. Equivalence is established by field-testing and explicit equating on real response data, and nothing in a static page can substitute for that.
  • That the decision rule is correct. Ardoin et al. reviewed 102 documents and found the CBM-R rules rest on expert opinion with very limited psychometric support. This page implements the convention faithfully; the convention is under-evidenced.
  • That the menu is the right menu. Its entries are plausible and drawn from the general shape of the intervention literature, but they are illustrative. A deployed system needs each entry tied to a specific evaluated intervention with a stated effect size, population and dosage, and needs a stopping rule that escalates to a human on repeated non-response.
  • That the simulated learner behaves like a child. Per-type Bernoulli accuracy with linear growth is a caricature. Real growth is non-linear, seasonal, and interacts with what is being taught that month.
  • That any of this teaches anyone anything. A demo shows a mechanism is buildable and coherent. It is not evidence that it teaches.
