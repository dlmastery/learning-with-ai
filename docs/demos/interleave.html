Demo 19 · Mechanisms

Blocked practice feels better and teaches less

Same items, same total practice, two orders. One of them produces visibly higher accuracy while the learner is working and lower accuracy a month later. Interleaving pools at g = 0.42 across 59 studies — and it is the one technique in the corpus whose sign depends on what you are practising, which this page lets you switch off with a slider.

400 learners simulated per run, in this page Hedges' g computed from the simulated samples A generative model, not measured data
What this is

A schedule simulator. One item pool, three problem types, two orders — AAA BBB CCC against ABC ACB CBA — run trial by trial through a learner model with four exposed parameters, then tested after a delay. Every number below, including the effect size, is produced by that run.

Why it matters

Blocked practice is what almost every worksheet, textbook section and AI-generated problem set does by default, because it is the obvious thing to do and because it produces the better-looking session. The classroom evidence against it is unusually good: 54 seventh-grade classes, four months, an unannounced test a month later, 61% versus 38%.

How to use it

The default run shows the signature. Then there is one parameter that matters more than the other three: confusability. Set it to zero — problem types that cannot be mistaken for one another — and the advantage does not shrink politely. It goes to roughly nothing and then negative, which is exactly what the vocabulary studies found.

1 · The two schedules

Blocked — all of A, then all of B, then all of C
Interleaved — one cycle at a time, order shuffled

2 · The parameters

The model is small enough to state in full. A learner has a strength per problem type and a single discrimination term — the ability to recognise which type a problem is when nothing tells them. In blocked practice the previous item tells them, so discrimination is never exercised and never grows.

How often the types can be mistaken for one another when the cue is removed. At 0 the learner always knows which procedure applies and discrimination is worth nothing. This is the moderator.
The acquisition penalty for changing task on consecutive trials — contextual interference. This is what interleaving pays, and it is why the trade can come out negative.
Time between the last practice trial and the test, in model days. At 0 the test is the practice, and blocked wins it.
Total practice is identical in both conditions — 3 types × this many. Only the order differs.

The divergence has a name here. The left-hand curve is the only evidence the learner has while deciding whether the method is working, and it is the curve that ranks the schedules backwards. This page does not re-argue that point — demo 08 takes it apart properly. What is worth noting is the mechanism, which is unusually plain: blocked practice removes the retrieval step. When the last four problems were all type B, the learner is not deciding what to do. They are executing.

4 · The moderator that decides the sign

Brunmair & Richter, 59 studies, 238 effect sizes: g = 0.67 for visual category induction, 0.34 for mathematics, not significant for expository texts and tastes, and g = −0.39 for vocabulary-like paired associates — where blocking wins, and not by a little. 5 · What this proves / what it does not

What this simulation earns
  • The crossover is not smuggled in. Nothing in the model says "interleaving is better". It says discrimination only grows on trials where the learner had to discriminate, spacing raises stability, and switching costs acquisition. Practice-versus-test reversal falls out of those three lines, which is the point of writing it as a generative model rather than a bar chart.
  • The moderator is load-bearing, and you can prove that by removing it. Drag confusability to zero and the pooled advantage collapses to approximately nothing and then goes negative under any switch cost. That reproduces the published sign flip — 0.67 for paintings, −0.39 for vocabulary — from a single parameter, without the parameter having been fitted to those numbers.
  • The statistic is real arithmetic. Hedges' g here is computed from the two simulated samples with the small-sample correction, not looked up. Change the seed by changing any parameter and it moves the way a sampled statistic should.
What it does not
  • This is a model, not a measurement, and the g it prints is not evidence. A generative model that reproduces a known pattern demonstrates that the pattern is mechanically coherent — that you do not need anything exotic to get it. It is not independent support for the pattern's existence. The support is Brunmair & Richter's 59 studies and Rohrer's cluster RCT; this page is an explanation of them, not a replication.
  • The parameter values are chosen, not estimated. Switch cost 0.30 and a 3-cycle spacing bonus are plausible magnitudes, not fitted ones. Read the ordering and the sign change; do not quote the numbers.
  • Real pools are not three clean types. The confusability slider is one number standing in for a similarity structure that is, in real material, a matrix — some pairs confusable, others not — and the right schedule for that is neither of the two here.
  • "Interleave everything" is the failure mode this page exists to prevent. A system applying interleaving as a global policy will improve mathematics practice and damage vocabulary learning in the same session, and the learner will report the vocabulary session as the good one. The policy has to be per-material, and the moderator is stated well enough to implement — which means there is no excuse for the global version.
