Demo 18 · The mechanisms

Generating items is free; knowing what they measure is not

The item bank was never valuable because items were scarce. It was valuable because calibration was scarce — and an item's parameters come from examinee responses, which generation does nothing to make free. Below, both halves run: the bank grows by fifty items a click, and the measurement properties arrive one respondent at a time.

Items generated and calibrated live Score-claim tier computed from bank state Shows a statistic that is undefined, not biased
What this is

An item bank with a truth underneath it. Every generated item carries latent parameters the page knows and the calibration does not; simulated learners answer; the estimates are recovered and scored against the truth they were never shown.

Why it matters

An infinite bank of uncalibrated items has no measurement properties at all. That sentence belongs on the wall of every institution rolling out a quiz generator. The binding constraint moved from authoring to a calibration sample, and almost every product built since has optimised the term that collapsed.

How to use it

Generate a few hundred items while holding the respondent count still. Precision does not stay flat — it degrades, because the same responses are now spread over more items. That single interaction is the whole argument, and it is the one thing a bigger bank cannot buy its way out of.

1 · The free half

One fine-grained content area — area of composite figures, grades 4–7, which is the grain size the economics is measured at. Items are generated from a cognitive model: each has structural features that cause its difficulty, and distractors derived from named student errors rather than from plausible-looking wrong numbers.

Generator
Seeded and deterministic — the same bank size reproduces the same bank.

2 · The half that is not free

Simulated learners with ability θ ~ N(0, 1) answer a 20-item probe drawn from the bank. Each response is a 2PL draw against the item's latent parameters; a wrong answer selects the distractor matching that learner's misconception with probability 0.6. Nothing is looked up — the estimates below are recovered from the response matrix alone.

Calibration sample
Responses per item is the quantity that matters, and it is respondents × probe length ÷ bank size. Growing the bank divides it.

3 · The inversion, priced

The only careful cost-benefit analysis of automatic item generation put the break-even in K-12 mathematics at 173 to 247 items within a single fine-grained content area. Template AIG has a large fixed cost — cognitive model, item model, tooling — and a near-zero marginal cost. Manual authoring is the reverse.

The term everyone optimises is the term that collapsed. The published cost model prices item development. It does not price calibration, review, or the ongoing security cost — and it did not need to, because before 2023 development dominated. Under LLM generation the development term falls to fractions of a cent and review becomes essentially 100% of the cost. The grey line is this page's construction rather than the published model's: it charges expert review and a calibration sample, both of which generation left exactly where they were. Note also what the shaded band means now. Template AIG's break-even is per content area, so a system covering 4,000 fine-grained objectives needed 4,000 × ~200 items before it paid — which is precisely why LLM generation, whose fixed cost is one prompt, is genuinely transformative and not merely faster. It moved the cheap term to zero.
And the bank saturates before you do. Generators show diminishing marginal syntactic return: a bank's effective item count is the number of psychometrically and syntactically distinguishable instances it can emit, and that number saturates well below the combinatorial count. A learner who sees thirty probes from one template has not made thirty independent observations. The item counter above is a raw count, and a raw count is the optimistic one.

4 · The statistic that stops existing

Cronbach's α needs a fixed number of items k, administered to a common sample, so that per-item variances and a total variance are estimable over the same people. If every learner sees a different item set, there is no item covariance matrix. Not a noisy one — no such object. α is not biased under generated assessment; it is undefined. McDonald's ω has the same structural requirement and dies the same way.

Co-observation, eight items
A filled cell is a pair of items that some common set of learners both saw, so a covariance is estimable. An empty cell is a covariance that does not exist.
What replaces it
Generalizability theory, which is native here: a generator is a formal specification of a universe of admissible observations — arguably the first time in the history of measurement that this universe has been written down executably rather than gestured at. Both quantities below are computed by administering the generator twice.
Report the pair (ρ_π, n_required(Φ)), never a single number and never α. ρ_π carries a version, because generators drift with model updates and prompt edits, and it is re-estimated on change. One incentive property of this design is worth keeping: a high-variance generator needs more probes for the same precision, which means a sloppy generator costs the learner time. That is the right direction for the pressure to point.

5 · Which claim this bank has actually earned

A system asserts a tier and must be able to produce the evidence for it. The tier is a property of the probe policy, not of the product. Toggle the evidence you have; the licence recomputes.

Most AI tutoring systems in 2026 sit at Tier 0 and report as if they were at Tier 2. That is the single most common measurement error in the field. And Tier 3 is not currently attainable by a purely generative system: the largest systematic review of AI-generated multiple-choice validity concludes the evidence "does not yet support unsupervised use in summative assessment." Say so rather than approximating it.

6 · What this proves, and where it stops

Demonstrated in the page
  • The two halves have opposite cost curves, and you can watch them fight. Adding two hundred items takes one click and degrades every parameter estimate in the bank, because responses per item is respondents × probe length ÷ bank size and only the denominator moved.
  • Calibration really is recoverable, and really does need a sample. The scatter converges on the diagonal at the rate the standard error predicts. Nothing about that is a mystery — it is the reason a calibration sample cannot be skipped, stated as arithmetic rather than as caution.
  • α's failure is structural and visible. The co-observation grid fills in under one fixed form and empties under per-learner generation. There is no estimator to fix, no correction to apply, and no sample size that rescues it.
  • The tier licence is decidable. Every requirement above is either satisfiable by evidence or not, and the page names the first unmet one instead of awarding a grade.
Where it stops
  • The learner is simulated and the truth is planted. Recovery is easy when the generating model and the fitting model are the same family. Real response data is not 2PL, and the honest version of this scatter is wider than the one above for reasons no simulation reproduces.
  • Isomorphicity is an assumption, and it has been tested. The load-bearing premise of item generation is that sibling items from one item model share parameters. When checked, only 9 of 23 expert-built templates produced psychometrically isomorphic instances without revision, and 9 of 23 required major modification. This page assumes it holds within a template; the field's own data says it holds about 40% of the time.
  • Every shipping system understates its measurement error. Adaptive scoring treats calibrated item parameters as known. Under generation they are draws, so the standard error of θ must include item-sampling variance, and no deployed system appears to do this. The prediction is falsifiable and cheap: reported reliabilities for LLM-generated adaptive quizzes are systematically optimistic, and the gap widens with item novelty. Duolingo's own published numbers show the size of it — test–retest 0.84 against internal consistency 0.96, and only one of those two is estimable under generation.
  • Fairness moves to the generator, and this page cannot check it. If every student receives distinct items, item-level differential functioning is undefined; what must be shown is generator invariance. Worse, personalised generation conditions item content on learner context, which can produce construct-irrelevant difficulty variation correlated with demographics — arising by design, invisible to every existing fairness procedure, and defended as a feature. That last risk is this project's own framing and no study of it was located. It is a hypothesis someone should test, and it is offered as the most serious unexamined fairness risk in AI-driven assessment.
