## VERDICT

**Not yet — and the gap between this and publishable is narrow, specific, and
entirely self-inflicted.** The prose is the best I have read from this kind of
project: the argument in `04-the-empty-chair.md`, `09-the-scoreboard.md`,
`11-the-archivist.md` and `17-showing.md` is real scholarship, and the standard the
document sets for others is the right standard. It fails its own standard in four
places at once. It publishes a superseded effect size on its front page as "the
honest field-wide number" one day after its own §09 documented the supersession. It
constructs a range — "0.63–0.73" — that appears in no source and splices two
different estimands. It quotes a 98.4% recognition figure from an exam-grading
benchmark as evidence that a tutor "sees" a child's handwritten work, two paragraphs
before explaining that it doesn't. And its public demo gallery advertises thirteen
demonstrations as running in your browser when twelve of the thirteen files do not
exist. A survey whose entire credibility claim is *we publish our own errors* cannot
ship with its own errors uncorrected at the point where they were made. Fix the
eleven items in the priority list and I would defend this. As it stands I would not
put my name on the front page, because the front page is the part that is wrong.

## THE WORST THING

**The corpus misquotes its own sources, and it does so specifically where the
misquotation flatters the argument.** Four instances, in ascending order of damage.

**1. Nickow 0.37 — the superseded number, republished as the honest one.**

`research/raw/B2-ai-tutoring-efficacy.md:99–100` records both estimates and the
lesson:

> | **Nickow, Oreopoulos & Quan (2024)**, *AERJ* | Pooled **0.288 SD (SE 0.029)** | **96 randomized studies** |
> | Same team, NBER w27476 (2020 working paper) | Pooled **0.37 SD** | Same 96 studies | **The number fell from 0.37 → 0.288 between working paper and peer review.** Treat all working-paper effect sizes accordingly |

`survey/09-the-scoreboard.md:205–208` repeats it as an editorial rule:

> "Nickow et al.'s human-tutoring pooled estimate **fell from 0.37 SD in the 2020
> working paper to 0.288 SD in the 2024 peer-reviewed version.** Discount every
> working-paper effect size accordingly, including the ones in the table above."

And then:

- `docs/index.html:455` — `{label:"Tutoring, pooled RCTs", note:"Nickow et al. — the honest field-wide number", v:0.37, ok:true}`
- `docs/index.html:191` — "Nickow's pooled tutoring RCTs land at 0.37."
- `docs/index.html:256` — "Does not replicate. **VanLehn 0.79 · Nickow 0.37 · Kestin 0.63–0.73**"
- `README.md:74` — "Pooled tutoring RCTs land at **0.37**"
- `README.md:202` — "**VanLehn 0.79 · Nickow 0.37**"
- `survey/03-the-vision.md:50` — "Nickow et al. pooled tutoring RCTs at **0.37 SD**"

Five publications of a working-paper figure the project has itself retired, one of
them labelled *the honest field-wide number*, and one of them plotted as a bar in a
chart. This is not a typo. It is the single behaviour the document spends 36,000
words condemning in other people, committed on its own front page. **Fix: 0.288
everywhere, with `(2020 working paper: 0.37)` as a parenthetical where the
working-paper/peer-review drift is the point being made.**

**2. Kestin "0.63–0.73" is a range that exists in no source.**

`research/raw/B2:64` and `:273–274`: "Regression d ≈ **0.63**, ceiling-corrected
**0.73–1.3**, p<10⁻⁸." Two estimands: an uncorrected regression estimate, and a
correction interval that *begins* where the survey's range *ends*. "0.63–0.73" takes
the point estimate of the first and the lower bound of the second and presents the
splice as an interval. It reads as a conservative confidence bound; it is neither
conservative nor a bound. It appears at `README.md:66`, `docs/index.html:150`,
`docs/index.html:256`, `survey/03-the-vision.md:51`, and — worst — is converted to a
false point estimate `v:0.68` and plotted at `docs/index.html:445`. Only
`survey/09-the-scoreboard.md:41` states it correctly ("d ≈ 0.63 (to 1.3
ceiling-corrected)"). **Fix: "d = 0.63; 0.73–1.3 after the authors' ceiling
correction" everywhere, and plot 0.63.**

Compounding it: "in 49 minutes against 60" (README:66, dashboard:150, 03:51) omits
that B2:274 says the 60 is *assumed*, not measured. And the tile sits in a section
headed *What is actually established* while B2:621 rates the study **(D)** —
first-author-built, first-author-analysed, no funding statement.

**3. 98.4% is quoted for a capability the same source says does not work.**

`survey/06-what-the-object-must-refuse.md:132–134`: "Handwriting and worked-page
recognition are at **98.4%** with false-negative rates driven to **0.58%**. The child
works on paper, with their hands, and the system sees it."

`research/raw/F7-A3:706–713`: 98.4% is **answer-position recognition on a benchmark
of 61 anonymised exams with 3,141 answer positions**, and the 0.58% false-negative
rate is achieved only by "a lightweight prompt that supplies **the reference solution
as context**" — i.e. the grader already knows the answer. The immediately preceding
paragraph (`:699–704`) reports that across nine VLMs, the best error-*correction*
rate is **77%**, and that "accuracy improves when handwritten inputs are replaced
with printed text." An effect quoted without its condition, generalised from a
structured-answer-position task to open-ended student work, three paragraphs before
the section correctly warns that VLMs silently repair the errors on that page. **Fix:
"98.4% on structured answer positions in an exam-grading benchmark, and 0.58% false
negatives only when the reference solution is supplied; on open student work the best
error-correction rate measured is 77%."**

**4. Spacing d = 0.54 is attributed to the wrong authors.**

`README.md:63`, `docs/index.html:149`, `survey/08:27` all give the base as "Cepeda
meta-analytic tradition." `research/raw/B1:120–122` gives the actual source:
"**Classroom meta-analysis (2025, PMC12189222)**: 22 reports, 31 effect sizes,
N > 3,000 … **d = 0.54, 95% CI [0.31, 0.77]**." Cepeda 2006 reports 271
massed-vs-spaced comparisons, not a d of 0.54. "Tradition" is not a citation; it is
the shape of a citation. In a document that publishes a rule saying a number must be
traceable, a number attributed to a *tradition* is a failure of the rule.

**Second-worst, and public right now:** `docs/demos/index.html` presents thirteen
demos, all tagged `Computed` — defined on that page as demos that "actually perform
the operation." `ls docs/demos/` returns `demo.css`, `index.html`,
`teachable-agent.html`. **Twelve of the thirteen links are 404s**, including
`patha.html`, `deixis.html`, `grounding-ladder.html`. `docs/index.html:395` sends
readers there with "thirteen techniques, **each running in the browser**."
`README.md:240` privately concedes "13 pages to build." The repo knows, and ships the
claim anyway.

## PEDAGOGICAL DEFECTS

**1. The K→doctorate matrix flattens exactly the axis it exists to distinguish.**
`docs/index.html:219–220` marks retrieval practice and spacing `core` in all five
bands, basis "g 0.50" / "d 0.54". That is the flattening the section's own callout
warns against. A five-year-old's retrieval practice is oral, single-item, immediately
scaffolded, and fails without corrective feedback (`B1:75`: "unsuccessful retrieval
without corrective feedback produces little or negative benefit" — the *modal* case in
K-2). A doctoral student's retrieval is self-generated over a literature. Pooling them
under one pill and one number is the error the document spends a whole section
condemning. **Fix: split the cell — `core (feedback mandatory)` for K-5,
`core (self-generated)` for grad — or drop the row's uniformity claim.**

**2. Two of the twelve matrix rows have no pedagogical evidence in the Basis column
at all.** "Genuine adversary — *unannounced only*" and "Open-ended research — *—*" are
rendered with the same pill vocabulary as rows carrying meta-analytic effects. A
reader cannot tell which cells are evidenced and which are the authors' judgement. In
a matrix whose whole function is to tell a builder what to do at each band, that is
the difference between guidance and a guess. **Fix: a fourth pill state, or an
explicit `judgement` marker on unevidenced cells.**

**3. `survey/04-the-empty-chair.md` — this is the strongest section in the corpus and
it is not wearing the subject as a costume.** A SELPA coordinator would recognise
their reality: the RTI regression-discontinuity result (§3), the opportunity-cost rule
for routing a child into intervention, the 7–10-week trend-validity floor, the
two-clock model, the refusal to build a working-memory trainer, and the four hard
limits in §5 (an AI may not author an IEP; may not diagnose; WCAG as floor; IDEA
destruction rights) are all things that come from having read the field rather than
having read about it. Two defects only, and both are small:

- §1's census is stated as "**Zero. Not 'few' — the intersection is empty**" and
  `H1:1363` records the limits ("searches title/abstract/keyword indexing in two
  databases"). A zero from abstract-level indexing is a zero in the *index*, not in
  the world, and the survey knows this — `survey/09:129` applies exactly that caveat
  to the ERIC counts ("ERIC indexes abstracts, not full text… these are lower
  bounds") and §04 does not. The strongest rhetorical claim in the document is the
  one place the caveat is dropped. **Fix: port the §09 sentence into §04 §1.**
- §2's "**two orders of magnitude larger**" compares 11 AI studies to 328 DI studies.
  That is 30×, not 100×. If the comparator is the ~4,000 effect estimates against 11
  studies the units are not comparable. **Fix: "the known-good intervention base is
  thirty times larger, and its effect-estimate base is four thousand against eleven."**

**4. The refusal engine is specified in a way that cannot be falsified.**
`survey/00:76–88` lists five inputs the tutor "must weigh" (has the learner attempted;
is the struggle productive; is this a retrieval opportunity; is frustration
approaching harm; is the method failing) with no thresholds, no measurement for any of
the five, and no stated failure condition. "Is frustration approaching the point of
harm?" is additionally unbuildable under the document's own §15 rule — Art. 5(1)(f)
prohibits affect inference in education, and §15 forbids a durable
`frustration_level`. The survey's most-repeated design commitment is currently a list
of questions nobody can answer with a measurement. **Fix: restate each input as an
observable with a threshold — attempts ≥ 1, latency since last input > *t*, consecutive
errors ≥ *n*, learner-stated signal — and mark the two that reduce to affect inference
as out of scope, citing §15.**

**5. `survey/03-the-vision.md` is where the discipline slips.** It is the only section
without a `## What this section commits us to` block, the only one without a
`source_report`, and the one carrying the superseded Nickow figure, the fabricated
Kestin range, "the floor with the brakes on," and "the entire history of education in
one story." That correlation is not an accident: the section that is not anchored to a
raw report is the section that drifts. **Fix: anchor it or cut it to half length.**

## THE SMELL TEST

**Would an elite educator believe a human expert wrote this? For most of it, yes —
and that is not a low bar being cleared.** The tells that usually give away generated
survey writing are absent: no hedged non-claims, no manufactured balance, no
vocabulary tells, no over-bolding of nothing (the bolding is dense but it lands on
numbers and named findings, which is legitimate), and the conclusions conclude rather
than summarise. Several passages could only have been written by someone who has been
in the room: the LearnLM tutor quote at `survey/09:243` ("As a lazy student, I'd have
loved it. As a tutor, not good at all!"), the RTI opportunity-cost argument, the
observation that fading too early and fading too late "look the same from the
outside."

**It breaks character in four places.**

1. **The section furniture.** Four sections closing with the same
   convergence-across-directions move, three with the identical heading "The nulls,
   given their own space," eleven with the identical `## What this section commits us
   to` bullet block. A human writing sixteen essays across four days does not
   converge on one skeleton; a template does. This is the single strongest generated-
   text signal in the corpus and it is cosmetic, which makes it cheap to fix and
   expensive to leave.
2. **The front page does not know what is behind it.** A human author who had just
   written 35,875 words does not publish "9 sections · 14,500 words," and does not
   write "31 research reports" over a directory containing 30. That specific
   failure — confident, precise, and unverified against the artifact three
   directories away — is the most recognisable signature of a document assembled by
   something that does not `ls`.
3. **The demo gallery.** No human ships thirteen cards marked *Computed* over twelve
   files that were never written. That is not over-claiming; it is not having looked.
4. **The uncorrected corrections.** An expert who caught themselves calling Sierra
   Leone "the strongest evidence in the history of educational technology" would
   delete the sentence. Appending a dated correction 100 lines below while the
   sentence stands is the behaviour of a process that only appends. It is also,
   precisely, the thing the README claims does not happen here.

The prose passes. The bookkeeping is what gives it away — and in a document whose
entire thesis is *check the number, check the condition, check what the corrigendum
actually said*, the bookkeeping is not a lesser category.
