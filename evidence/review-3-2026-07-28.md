The mechanism is the C-6/C-12 rule, `:30`:

```python
r"\bNickow[^.]{0,80}?0\.37\b|0\.37[^.]{0,60}?Nickow"
```

`[^.]` excludes the full stop. The literal string in the repository was `Nickow et al. —
… v:0.37`. The period in *"et al."* terminates the character class two characters after
`Nickow`, so the alternation can never reach `0.37`. **The rule written from C-12 cannot
match the sentence that produced C-12.** The same class kills the bare-prose forms: a
`0.37` in a sentence whose subject was named in the previous sentence is invisible, and
that is how effect sizes are actually written. Sentence-scoped rules for a corpus whose
errors are chart data and cross-sentence prose.

**Which corrections have no rule and should.** Twelve rules cover thirteen ledger rows:
C-1, C-2, C-4, C-6/C-12, C-7, C-13, C-15, C-16, C-18, C-19, C-20, C-22. Uncovered:
C-3, C-5, C-8, C-9, C-10, C-11, C-14, C-17, C-21, C-23. C-17 and C-23 are process
corrections and cannot be ruled — fair. The rest are substantive value corrections that
should have rules, and two of them matter right now:

- **C-21 has no rule, and its violation is live.** The ledger says two harnesses measured
  0.38 ms and 0.61 ms and *"both are now reported rather than the flattering one."*
  `docs/index.html:231` still publishes **`99.1% @ 0.61 ms`** — the splice
  `docs/demos/grounding-ladder.html:167–170` holds up as the thing not to do, and
  `survey/13:119` gives 0.38 ms median. Flagged in review 1, in review 2 (item 11), still
  there. **The checker is green while a ledgered correction is being violated on the most
  prominent surface in the project.**
- **C-8 has no rule, and its violation is live.** `survey/01:149` still reads
  *"**Gap-widening is the default expectation, not a risk.**"* with no untargeted-delivery
  qualifier, third round running, while `survey/09:284` carries it correctly.

**Is the 600-character window a real check or a loophole?** The window is real — I
verified the boundary: a cure at 400 characters passes, at 700 it fails. It would have
caught `survey/15`'s 67-line-distant correction. **The loophole is not the window, it is
the cure vocabulary.** C-1's cure is `was our error|corrected|not significant|\+0\.216`
and C-22's is `eight weeks|8 weeks|corrected`. The bare word *"corrected"* cures both. I
demonstrated it: a live, uncorrected *"strongest evidence in the history of educational
technology"* followed 500 characters later by *"the spacing attribution was **corrected**
to a 2025 classroom meta-analysis"* — an unrelated correction about a different number —
is scored as cured. In a corpus that discusses its own corrections on nearly every page,
`corrected` is close to a universal solvent.

**And nothing runs it.** `grep -rn check-corrections` across the repo returns exactly two
hits, both in `evidence/README-tests.md`. There is no CI, no hook, no pre-commit gate.
Meanwhile `.autosave.sh` commits and pushes **every 180 seconds unconditionally**. The
publish path bypasses the gate every three minutes. Review 2 asked for *"a single
pre-publish grep that fails the build."* What shipped is a script that fails to detect,
and is never invoked.

**Fixes, in order:** (a) replace `[^.]{0,80}` with a windowed proximity search over
normalised text so `et al.` and chart literals cannot break a rule — the tool already has
a windowing primitive, it just does not use it for the *bad* pattern; (b) add a bare
`\bv:\s*0\.37\b`-class rule for chart data, which is where two of the three worst
findings lived; (c) make cure regexes specific to the correction (`0\.288` for C-12, not
`superseded`; `eight weeks` for C-22, not `corrected`); (d) add rules for C-8, C-14,
C-21; (e) add a count rule — see fix 5; (f) run it in `.autosave.sh` before `git push`,
`--strict`, and refuse to push on non-zero.

## IS THE CENTRAL CLAIM EARNED

The claim, stated at `survey/20:173–176` and `survey/03:174`: the measured 0.2–0.4 SD
band is *"the floor with the brakes on — that constrained, grounded, pivoting,
remembering systems would do better, and that nobody has built the good version and
measured it."*

**It is two claims, and they have opposite verdicts.**

### The second half is earned, and it is the best thing this project has done

*Nobody has built and measured it* is not a hope. It is a censused absence, and the
census is the strongest scholarship in the corpus:

- `survey/23` — 2,907 arXiv papers, ≤ 1.79% carrying a learning outcome, **eight
  subfields at zero**, and the refusal to sensationalise it (*"That is not a scandal and
  we are not going to write it as one. It is a phase."*).
- `survey/04` — zero randomised trials of generative-AI tutoring on learners with
  disabilities, across two databases, censused twice.
- `README.md:222` — ERIC returns **0 records** for `"retention test" AND "ChatGPT"`. The
  primary outcome that 17 of 19 proposed experiments require has never been measured in
  this literature.
- `F9`'s nineteen experiment designs, each with a power justification and a
  pre-registered falsifier.

A field census that names what has *not* been measured, with queries a reader can re-run,
is a genuine contribution. I would publish this half on its own.

### The first half is a hope, and I can say exactly where the smuggling happens

*0.2–0.4 SD is a floor, not a ceiling* is an inference from an absence dressed as an
inference from evidence. Check what actually supports it:

1. **Bastani's −17% → −0.004.** This is the single load-bearing datum, and **the project's
   own C-2 correction disarms it**: *"Guardrails **remove harm**. The guardrailed arm's
   unassisted coefficient is −0.004, n.s. Harm removed; benefit not demonstrated."* A
   constraint that moves an arm from −17% to zero is evidence that a design failure can be
   undone. It is not evidence that anything rises above 0.2–0.4. The survey corrected this
   number itself and then continued to reason from the uncorrected version.
2. **VanLehn 0.79 / 0.76.** These are the strongest numbers in the ladder, and **they cut
   against the thesis, which the survey never confronts.** Pre-LLM intelligent tutoring
   systems were constrained, grounded, and had persistent learner models — four of the five
   properties the claim says nobody has built. They landed at 0.76 in VanLehn's synthesis
   and the pooled randomised figure across 96 trials is **0.288**. The best available
   natural experiment on "does adding constraint and grounding raise the floor" already ran,
   for thirty years, and pooled at 0.288. Neither `survey/20`'s five premises nor
   `survey/03`'s bet mentions this. **It is the strongest counter-argument available and it
   is absent from a section titled "What would falsify this survey."**
3. **Kestin d ≈ 0.63.** Developer-built, developer-evaluated, no funding statement, rated
   (D) by the project's own `B2:621`.

Against it, from the survey's own pages: `survey/20`'s **Premise 3** — *"Every one of
those is a case where the elaborated version lost to the plain one"*, five instances, all
internal; **Premise 4** — with teacher support g = 1.426, without g = 0.077; **Premise 5**
— *"Where independence and rigour increase, effects shrink."*

**`survey/20` does not smuggle.** It states the counter-case in five premises, labels its
own replies *"as reasons to test, not refutations"*, and publishes three falsification
conditions in advance. That section is the claim handled correctly, and it is why I can
answer the question at all.

**The smuggling is in three places, and all three are the reader-facing ones.**

- **`survey/03:174` — the primary smuggler.** *"That is the floor with the brakes on."*
  Flat, unhedged, no cross-reference to §20 or §21, in a section headed **"5. The bet"**.
  It is also the section with `status: draft`, **no `source_report`**, and no
  `## What this section commits us to` block — review 1 identified that correlation
  ("the section that is not anchored to a raw report is the section that drifts"),
  review 2 repeated it, and nothing changed. The section carrying the survey's central
  claim is the one section not anchored to evidence.
- **`README.md:33–34`** — *"a system that works at the margin works everywhere"* stated as
  the organising principle, while `survey/04` calls that exact clause *"a design
  heuristic, **not a finding**."* Third round unchanged.
- **`docs/index.html`** — states the counter-case honestly at `:402` and then publishes
  `Real-time is solved` at `:292`, `99.1% @ 0.61 ms` at `:231`, and a 26-row ladder that
  pools meta-analytic estimates, single trials and non-effects on one axis with no `kind`
  field.

**Verdict: half-earned.** *Nobody has measured it* is proven. *It would be better* is a
hypothesis the survey is entitled to hold, entitled to build on, and **not** entitled to
state as a finding on its front page — which is the distinction this document spends
60,000 words enforcing on everyone else.

**The fix is one sentence and one paragraph.** Change `survey/03:174` to §20's own
framing, and add VanLehn's ITS result to §20's premises as Premise 6, because it is the
best argument against the thesis and the survey is currently the only party not making
it. Adding it would make the claim *stronger*, not weaker — a thesis that survives its
best counter-argument in print is worth more than one that never met it.
