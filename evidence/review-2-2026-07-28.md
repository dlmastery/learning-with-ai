```python
CARD = re.compile(r'<(a|div) class="card[^"]*"(?: href="([^"]+)")?(?: data-demo="([^"]+)")?>(.*?)</\1>', re.S)
```

contains:

```html
  <a class="card" href="learner-model.html" data-demo="learner-model.html">
```

`<span class="tag …">` sat outside the truncated body it was never rewritten. Identical damage at

### `grounding-ladder.html` — the claim is true, and stronger than advertised

Verified by running lines 254–641 in Node against a stubbed DOM. There is no recorded result in the
file. The page contains, in order: a hand-written tokeniser and recursive-descent parser
(`P`, lines 259–278) with correct precedence and right-associative `^`; a dimensional-analysis engine
over exponent maps with proper error cases (`dim`, 309–329) that rejects adding `[M·L²·T⁻²]` to
`[L]` and rejects dimensional exponents; a canonical-form comparator (`canon`, 353–378) doing
constant folding, power collection, small-integer expansion and opaque-function wrapping; a seeded
LCG (`rnd`, 423) so the corpus is reproducible; and five real AST mutators.

Measured output, k = 1, narrow domain, this machine:

```
20 formulas · 91 cases · 73 true mutants · 18 cases where pass is correct
                                           (11 null mutants + 7 identity rewrites)
L1 dimensional  30.1%   22/73 caught · 0/18 false alarms ·   504 ns
L2 numeric     100.0%   73/73 caught · 0/18 false alarms ·   165 ns
L3 structural  100.0%   73/73 caught · 1/18 false alarms ·   4.9 µs
```

The k-sweep reproduces the null (73/73 at every k from 1 to 16, cost 1.00× → 3.53×) and, on the
signed domain, reproduces the *sharper* claim the page makes about it (94.5% at k = 1 climbing to
100% at k = 8) — so the assertion that "k is a subsidy for a badly-declared domain" is genuinely
measured, not asserted.

**The false alarm is real and self-inflicted.** The one flagged row is `ln(a*b)` vs `ln(a) + ln(b)`,
L3 = *flagged*, and it is flagged because `canon` treats `ln` as an opaque symbol
(`opaque(n.n+'{'+cstr(canon(n.a))+'}')`, line 360), so `ln{a¹·b¹}` ≠ `ln{a} + ln{b}`. Nothing
special-cases it into existence and nothing suppresses it. It is disclosed twice — once in the
false-alarm table it computes, and once at line 232–235: *"It does not do trigonometric identities,
logarithm laws or partial fractions — and it tells you so, in the false-alarm table, by getting the
log rule wrong in front of you."* That is a checker publishing its own failure mode as a first-class
output, and it is the single most credible thing in the repository.

**One overclaim, and it is in the "What it proves" list.** Line 218–220:

> "Sampling outside the declared domain also produces false alarms, on expressions that are
> genuinely equal … **This reproduces the corpus's ordering (0 narrow, 1 wide, 2 signed) from
> independent code.**"

It does not. Measured L2 false alarms at k = 1, all three domains: **0, 0, 0**. Across the full
sweep, wide is 0 at every k; signed is 0 at k = 1, then 2, 2, 1, 2 — non-monotone, and never
matching the corpus's 0/1/2 (`survey/13:180–182`). Line 634 asserts the same equivalence in a
conditional note. A claim of independent reproduction, in a bullet list headed *what this page
proves*, on a page whose banner reads *"There is no recorded result anywhere on this page."*
**Fix:** change to "The corpus reports 0/1/2 for narrow/wide/signed; this page's corpus is smaller
and different and does not reproduce that ordering — it reproduces only the direction."

Minor: the gallery card hardcodes *"L2 100% recall @ 170 ns"* — a machine-specific measurement in a
static card, on the one page that goes out of its way to say your numbers will differ.

### `adversary.html` — arithmetic correct, distinctness genuinely measured

`RADIX` = 6 × 12 × 6 × 6 × 5 × 3 × 4 × 3 × 2 × 2 × 2 × 2 × 2. Computed: **14,929,920.** ✓ The product
is taken from the table the page prints, so the number and its derivation cannot drift apart.

Distinctness is measured, not asserted. Lines 439–453 draw 20,000 indices, build the full item for
each via `itemOf`, hash on substantive content, and count the set. Executed: **19,932 / 20,000 =
99.66%** — matching the gallery card exactly. And the page explains its own shortfall correctly
(*"when the question asks for the capacity, the stated capacity digit is unused, and when the second
agent drains, some rate digits are clamped"*), then concludes *"A generator that quotes its space
size without measuring its collisions is quoting a ceiling."* Correct, and rare.

Two small things. The sample is drawn from a **fixed-seed LCG** (`s=987654321`), so *"20,000 indices
were drawn at random just now"* is deterministic and identical on every load — say "a fixed
20,000-index sample" and keep the reproducibility, which is the better property anyway. And the
birthday bound (`Math.round(Math.sqrt(2*SPACE*0.001))` = 173) is computed against the *ceiling* two
paragraphs before the page argues the effective space is smaller; label it "against the nominal
space."

### `teachable-agent.html` — no special-casing

Verified by reading. `parse` (lines 212–228) is a genuine recursive-descent parser —
`expr → term → un → atom`, correct precedence, balanced-paren checking, useful error messages. `ev`
walks the AST. The truth is exact integer rational arithmetic (`truthN = a*d+c*b`, `truthD = b*d`)
and equality is tested by **cross-multiplication** (`pn*truthD === truthN*pd`), not float comparison.

The rule is applied uniformly to all eight suite items with no branch on which rule was taught, no
lookup table of known-wrong rules, and no fallback. The "half a pizza" answer at lines 380–392 is
derived from the same `ev` call on `{a:1,b:2,c:1,d:2}` — including the branch where a shrinking rule
makes the protégé assert *"Adding a half to a half makes it smaller."* The claim holds.

The `lucky` computation (365–372) — printing which items a broken rule still passes, and concluding
*"A suite made only of items like `0/1 + 0/1` would have certified this rule as correct"* — is a
piece of assessment design most psychometrics courses do not reach.

Two nits: the parser is ~18 lines, not "~60"; and `ev` divides in floating point, so a user-typed
`a/b` rule would break the exact-equality claim. Neither is a fabrication.

## VOICE AND SEAMS

The three writing teams are more alike than I expected, and the seam does not show where I looked
for it — it shows in the furniture.

**Where the prose holds.** `20-the-agenda` opens on two ERIC queries returning zero and lets the
table do the work. `18-the-textbook-that-writes-itself` opens on a timestamped GitHub commit —
*"twenty chapters, roughly 57,000 words … published in two commits, thirty-eight minutes apart …
`authors = ["OpenAI Codex with Xiaol"]`"* — and the specificity is doing argumentative work, not
decorative work. `23` opens by admitting it previously mislabelled its own finding as a local
curiosity. Set beside `04`'s *"Start with a specific child, because the general case is where this
field goes to avoid the work,"* these are recognisably the same standard. Sentence rhythm, bolding
density and the willingness to end a section on an unresolved question are consistent. I could not
reliably assign sections to authors by voice, and I tried.

**Where the seam shows: the skeleton, not the sentences.** 19 of 24 sections close on
`## N. What this section commits us to`. Six now carry `## The nulls, given their own space` — up
from three, because the new sections copied it. Round 1 called this "the single strongest generated-
text signal in the corpus," and the response was to reproduce it in twelve more files. A human
writing twenty-four essays over four days does not converge on one skeleton; a template does. It is
cosmetic, which is exactly why leaving it is expensive: it costs credibility the prose has already
earned.

**Where 22 does not sound like 04.** `22:11–15` is the one lede that grades itself against its
neighbours (*"This one asks the harder question, and the one everybody actually wants answered"*) and
then substitutes antithesis for evidence (*"The answer is not an algorithm. It is a fifteen-second
measurement"*). §04 never tells you it is important; it shows you an eleven-year-old and lets you
conclude it. That is the seam, and it is one paragraph wide — §22's §1, *"Start with the failure,
because it is fifty years long"*, immediately recovers the register.

**Repetition across sections: better than I expected, and I want this on the record.** Bastani's
−17% appears 23 times across 8 of 24 sections. I checked every instance. None laundered it as a new
finding, and several *sharpened* it on reuse: `20:29–31` gives the raw coefficients (−0.054, SE 0.022
vs −0.004, SE 0.013) rather than restating the percentage; `12:373–375` volunteers the PNAS-erratum
provenance unprompted; `04:193–195` restates it with the harm-removed/benefit-not-demonstrated
qualifier attached; `23:97` files it in a table of nulls. This is the reuse discipline working. The
only cost is that no instance says "as §01 established," so a skimmer counting distinct findings will
over-count. One cross-reference each would fix it.

## WHAT IS ACTUALLY GOOD

Short, and I would defend every line.

- **`grounding-ladder.html`.** A parser, a dimensional engine and a canonical-form comparator written
  from scratch, running in a static page, timed on the reader's own hardware, reporting a false alarm
  the author's own comparator commits and then explaining why. I ran it. It does what it says. This
  is the best artifact in the repository and I do not know of another survey that ships one.
- **`adversary.html`'s refusal to say "unbounded."** Computing 14,929,920 from a printed radix table,
  then measuring 99.66% distinctness and explaining the 0.34% shortfall, then writing *"A generator
  that quotes its space size without measuring its collisions is quoting a ceiling"* — that is the
  editorial standard applied to the project's own marketing copy.
- **The EU AI Act correction** (`survey/15:12–24`). Names the instrument, the OJ citation, the new
  date, and the article that did *not* move. Most compliance writing gets the Article 50 carve-out
  wrong. Verified against three surfaces and consistent on all three.
- **`survey/20-the-agenda`.** Nineteen experiments is the right number to specify and *"A variable
  that moves an outcome from −17% to zero is not near a ceiling; it is near a decision"* is the
  sentence the field's funders need. §20's willingness to put the survey's own thesis on the list —
  *"Does the guardrail ever add benefit?"* — is the standard working.
- **`survey/23`'s denominator.** 2,907 arXiv papers, ≤1.79% with a learning outcome, eight subfields
  at zero — and then refusing the easy conclusion: *"That is not a scandal and we are not going to
  write it as one. It is a phase."* Followed by crediting Khan Academy for publishing a null against
  interest. That is how you write a field census without becoming a scold.
- **`survey/08:33–38`.** The retrieval-practice boundary conditions, stated in the same breath as the
  effect size, without hedging the recommendation. Textbook.
- **The slope chart's honest demotion.** `renderSlope` now draws a dotted connector with the comment
  *"connector encodes PAIRING only — never magnitude"* and an `aria-label` reading *"The two columns
  use different units and share no scale."* Round 1 said the chart's visual channel encoded nothing;
  the response was to say so on the chart rather than fake an encoding. That is the right call and
  most people would have faked the encoding.
- **`survey/12:373–375`** volunteering the PNAS-erratum provenance in a parenthesis nobody would have
  noticed was missing.
