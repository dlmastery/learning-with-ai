```python
CARD = re.compile(r'<(a|div) class="card[^"]*"(?: href="([^"]+)")?(?: data-demo="([^"]+)")?>(.*?)</\1>', re.S)
```

For a `<div class="card soon">` the non-greedy body terminates at the **first** `</div>` — the inner
`<div class="n">05</div>`. So the script emitted, and `docs/demos/index.html:105–106` now contains:

```html
  <a class="card" href="learner-model.html" data-demo="learner-model.html">
    <div class="n">05</a>
```

The anchor closes after two characters; the title, description and tag fall outside it. Because the
`<span class="tag …">` sat outside the truncated body it was never rewritten. Identical damage at
`:159`, `:168`, `:177`, `:186` — `pivot-loop`, `distractors`, `patha`, `cbm-probe` — and `:103`
closes an `<a>` with `</div>`, terminating the `.grid` early.

### `grounding-ladder.html` — the claim is true, and stronger than advertised

Verified by running lines 254–641 in Node against a stubbed DOM. There is no recorded result in the
file. It contains, in order: a hand-written tokeniser and recursive-descent parser (`P`, 259–278)
with correct precedence and right-associative `^`; a dimensional-analysis engine over exponent maps
with real error cases (`dim`, 309–329) that rejects adding `[M·L²·T⁻²]` to `[L]` and rejects
dimensional exponents; a canonical-form comparator (`canon`, 353–378) doing constant folding, power
collection, small-integer expansion and opaque-function wrapping; a seeded LCG (423) making the
corpus reproducible; and five real AST mutators.

Measured, k = 1, narrow domain, on this machine:

```
20 formulas · 91 cases · 73 true mutants · 18 cases where pass is correct
                                           (11 null mutants + 7 identity rewrites)
L1 dimensional  30.1%   22/73 caught · 0/18 false alarms ·   504 ns
L2 numeric     100.0%   73/73 caught · 0/18 false alarms ·   165 ns
L3 structural  100.0%   73/73 caught · 1/18 false alarms ·   4.9 µs
```

The k-sweep reproduces the null (73/73 at every k from 1 to 16, cost 1.00× → 3.53×) and, on the
signed domain, reproduces the *sharper* claim the page makes about it (94.5% at k = 1 climbing to
100% at k = 8) — so *"k is a subsidy for a badly-declared domain"* is measured, not asserted.

**The false alarm is real and self-inflicted.** The one flagged row is `ln(a*b)` vs `ln(a) + ln(b)`,
L3 = *flagged*, because `canon` treats `ln` as opaque (line 360), so `ln{a¹·b¹}` ≠ `ln{a} + ln{b}`.
Nothing conjures it and nothing suppresses it. It is disclosed twice — in the false-alarm table it
computes, and at `:232–235`: *"It does not do trigonometric identities, logarithm laws or partial
fractions — and it tells you so, in the false-alarm table, by getting the log rule wrong in front of
you."* A checker publishing its own failure mode as a first-class output. This is the most credible
artifact in the repository.

**One overclaim, and it is in the "What it proves" list.** `:218–220`:

> "Sampling outside the declared domain also produces false alarms … **This reproduces the corpus's
> ordering (0 narrow, 1 wide, 2 signed) from independent code.**"

It does not. Measured L2 false alarms at k = 1: **0, 0, 0** across all three domains. Across the full
sweep, wide is 0 at every k; signed is 0, 2, 2, 1, 2 — non-monotone, never matching the corpus's
0/1/2 (`survey/13:180–182`). `:634` asserts the same equivalence in a conditional note. A claim of
independent reproduction, in a list headed *what this page proves*, on a page whose banner reads
*"There is no recorded result anywhere on this page."* Also: the gallery card hardcodes *"L2 100%
recall @ 170 ns"*, a machine-specific number, on the one page that insists your numbers will differ.

### `adversary.html` — arithmetic correct, distinctness genuinely measured

`RADIX` = 6 × 12 × 6 × 6 × 5 × 3 × 4 × 3 × 2 × 2 × 2 × 2 × 2 = **14,929,920** ✓, and the product is
taken from the table the page prints, so number and derivation cannot drift apart.

Distinctness is measured. Lines 439–453 draw 20,000 indices, build each full item via `itemOf`, hash
on substantive content, count the set. Executed: **19,932 / 20,000 = 99.66%** — matching the gallery
card exactly. The page then explains its own shortfall correctly (*"when the question asks for the
capacity, the stated capacity digit is unused, and when the second agent drains, some rate digits are
clamped"*) and concludes *"A generator that quotes its space size without measuring its collisions is
quoting a ceiling."*

Two small things. The sample comes from a **fixed-seed LCG** (`s=987654321`), so *"20,000 indices
were drawn at random just now"* is deterministic and identical every load — say "a fixed 20,000-index
sample" and keep the reproducibility, which is the better property. And the birthday bound (173) is
computed against the *ceiling* two paragraphs before the page argues the effective space is smaller;
label it "against the nominal space."

### `teachable-agent.html` — no special-casing

`parse` (212–228) is a genuine recursive-descent parser — `expr → term → un → atom`, correct
precedence, balanced-paren checking, useful errors. Truth is exact integer rational arithmetic
(`truthN = a*d+c*b`, `truthD = b*d`) and equality is tested by **cross-multiplication**
(`pn*truthD === truthN*pd`), not float comparison.

The rule is applied uniformly to all eight suite items with no branch on which rule was taught, no
table of known-wrong rules, no fallback. The "half a pizza" answer (380–392) comes from the same `ev`
call on `{a:1,b:2,c:1,d:2}`, including the branch where a shrinking rule makes the protégé assert
*"Adding a half to a half makes it smaller."* The claim holds.

The `lucky` computation (365–372) — printing which items a broken rule still passes, concluding *"A
suite made only of items like `0/1 + 0/1` would have certified this rule as correct"* — is a piece of
assessment design most psychometrics courses never reach.

Nits: the parser is ~18 lines, not "~60"; `ev` divides in floating point, so a user-typed `a/b` rule
would break the exact-equality claim. Neither is a fabrication.

## VOICE AND SEAMS

The three writing teams are more alike than I expected, and the seam does not show where I looked for
it. It shows in the furniture and in the arithmetic.

**Where the prose holds.** `20-the-agenda` opens on two ERIC queries returning zero and lets the table
argue. `18-the-textbook-that-writes-itself` opens on a timestamped commit — *"twenty chapters,
roughly 57,000 words … published in two commits, thirty-eight minutes apart … `authors = ["OpenAI
Codex with Xiaol"]`"* — and the specificity does argumentative work. `23` opens by admitting it had
previously mislabelled its own finding as a local curiosity. Set beside `04`'s *"Start with a
specific child, because the general case is where this field goes to avoid the work,"* these are
recognisably one standard. **I could not reliably assign sections to authors by voice, and I tried.**

**Where the seam shows: the skeleton.** 19 of 24 sections close on `## N. What this section commits
us to`. Six carry `## The nulls, given their own space` — up from three, because the new sections
copied it; two more carry near-variants (`16:281`, `19:179`). Five carry the identical heading `The
strongest counter-argument`, and each opens with the same device: an italicised imagined objector
followed by a two-part rebuttal (`09:251`, `11:338`, `12:356`, `13:396`, `14:417`). Round 1 called
the templating "the single strongest generated-text signal in the corpus," and the response was to
reproduce it in twelve more files. A human writing twenty-four essays over four days does not
converge on one skeleton; a form does. It is cosmetic, which is precisely why leaving it is
expensive: it costs credibility the prose has already earned.

**Where 22 does not sound like 04.** `22:11–15` is the one lede that grades itself against its
neighbours and then substitutes antithesis for evidence. §04 never tells you it is important; it
shows you an eleven-year-old. The seam is one paragraph wide — §22's *"Start with the failure,
because it is fifty years long"* immediately recovers the register. `21:11` and `22:11` also open
with the identical formula (*"Every section before this one …"*), which neither baseline section uses.

**Where 20 and 21 diverge structurally.** `20` abandons numbered `##` sections entirely and leaves its
final heading unnumbered, alone among the 24. `21` carries **zero tables and zero blockquotes** in a
survey where every other section uses both, and hedges six times (`:44`, `:95`, `:100`, `:113`,
`:178`) where `09` hedges once and backs it with a table. Read `21` and `09` consecutively and the
seam is immediate.

**Repetition across sections: better than I expected, and it belongs on the record.** Bastani's −17%
appears 23 times across 8 of 24 sections. I checked every instance. **None laundered it as a new
finding**, and several *sharpened* it on reuse: `20:29–31` gives raw coefficients (−0.054, SE 0.022
vs −0.004, SE 0.013) rather than restating the percentage; `04:193–195` attaches the
harm-removed/benefit-not-demonstrated qualifier; `23:97` files it in a table of nulls; and `09:157`
is the only place in the corpus that says the quiet part — *"This survey has stated the consequence
before and states it again without softening it."* That is the reuse discipline working.

The exception is exact duplication presented twice as a fresh act of transparency: `09:162–164` and
`12:373–375` carry the same parenthetical — *"A note on provenance … the PNAS notice attached to
[Bastani] is an affiliation erratum. It is not a correction to the result. The −17% stands"* — in
near-identical words, each framed as a first disclosure, neither referencing the other. Same pattern
at `20:278` / `21:274` (the delivery thesis) and `20:253` / `21:269` (the concession conditions),
where the second instance is offered as an independently reached concession and both then cite `04`
as corroboration. **One source counted three times reads as convergence and is not.**

## WHAT IS ACTUALLY GOOD

Short, and I would defend every line.

- **`grounding-ladder.html`.** A parser, a dimensional engine and a canonical-form comparator written
  from scratch, running in a static page, timed on the reader's hardware, reporting a false alarm the
  author's own comparator commits and then explaining why. I ran it; it does what it says. I do not
  know of another survey that ships one.
- **`adversary.html`'s refusal to say "unbounded."** Computing 14,929,920 from a printed radix table,
  measuring 99.66% distinctness, explaining the 0.34% shortfall, then writing *"A generator that
  quotes its space size without measuring its collisions is quoting a ceiling."* The editorial
  standard turned on the project's own marketing copy.
- **The EU AI Act correction** (`survey/15:12–24`). The instrument, the OJ citation, the new date, and
  the article that did *not* move. Most compliance writing gets the Article 50 carve-out wrong.
- **`survey/20-the-agenda`.** Nineteen experiments is the right number to specify, *"A variable that
  moves an outcome from −17% to zero is not near a ceiling; it is near a decision"* is the sentence
  the field's funders need, and putting *"Does the guardrail ever add benefit?"* — the survey's own
  thesis — on the list is the standard working.
- **`survey/23`'s denominator.** 2,907 arXiv papers, ≤1.79% with a learning outcome, eight subfields
  at zero, then refusing the easy conclusion: *"That is not a scandal and we are not going to write it
  as one. It is a **phase**."* Followed by crediting Khan Academy for publishing a null against
  interest. That is how to write a field census without becoming a scold.
- **`survey/19:212–219`.** Publishing the falsification of the project's own pāṭha proposal — *"The
  idea was good, the mechanism was clearly stated, and the measurement said no. That is how this is
  supposed to work"* — with no hedging and no face-saving.
- **`survey/08:33–38`.** The retrieval-practice boundary conditions stated in the same breath as the
  effect size, without weakening the recommendation.
- **The slope chart's honest demotion.** `renderSlope` now draws a dotted connector commented
  *"connector encodes PAIRING only — never magnitude"* and an `aria-label` reading *"The two columns
  use different units and share no scale."* Round 1 said the visual channel encoded nothing; the
  response was to say so on the chart rather than fake an encoding. Most people would have faked it.
