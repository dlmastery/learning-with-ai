## IS THE AGENTIC RULE TRUE

> *"The value of an agentic loop equals the value of the external check it closes on."*

**As a heuristic, it is the most useful sentence in the paper. As stated, it is false,
and the section's own evidence table is what makes it look true.**

**Where it holds.** The four-way decomposition — sampling, execution, persistence,
absence — is correct and it is not a list, because each term is genuinely a multiplier
with a named degenerate case: *"Sampling without a selector is noise. Execution without
a test is output. Persistence without a schema is a transcript. Absence without a
verifier is unsupervised drift."* That is a real analysis and I have not seen it
compressed that well. The prescriptive form is unimpeachable: *"If you cannot say what
plays the role of `pytest`, you have built a chatbot with extra steps."* And the
tutoring application is measured, not asserted: across **223 real ITS domains, no model
beat chance at labelling an incorrect student action** (`K2:1104–1132`, TutorGym,
arXiv:2505.01563). That single result does more to justify the survey's modesty than
any argument in it.

**Where it fails, and the failure is in the table at `:45–53`.** The section prints:

| Benchmark | Score | Is there a check? |
|---|---|---|
| SWE-bench Verified | 79.2% | Yes — `pytest` |
| Terminal-Bench 2.1 | 83.8% | Yes |
| PaperBench | 21.0% | **No** |
| SciCode | 4.6% | **No** |

`K2:53–58`, its own source, gives a **three-level gradient**:

> `| Research-grade scientific code | **Weakly** — hand-written tests over novel science | 4.6% |`
> `| Replicating a research paper | **No** — rubric graded | 21.0% |`

**SciCode has hand-written tests and scores 4.6%.** It is the counter-example to the
rule, it is in the source, and the section rewrote *"Weakly — hand-written tests"* as
*"No."* PaperBench is graded against **8,316 individually gradable rubric items
co-developed with the original authors** (`K2:234–239`) — an external check, and an
expensive one. Rendering both as a flat *No* is what produces the clean twenty-fold
spread the rule is inferred from. **A survey that killed `0.63–0.73` for splicing two
estimands cannot flatten its own source's three-level column into a binary in the
direction that flatters its thesis.**

**Three further reasons the rule is a slogan in its strong form.**

1. **`:52` asserts the counterfactual it needs and does not test it** — *"the axis is
   not how hard the task is."* SWE-bench *Verified* is the human-filtered subset
   selected for solvability. PaperBench is *replicate an ICML Spotlight from scratch*.
   Difficulty and checkability are confounded across these four rows and the section
   waves the confound away in one clause. This is the difference-of-conditions error
   the paper polices in everyone else.
2. **The section's own §4 contains three counter-examples.** *"PaperQA2 matches or
   exceeds subject-matter experts, with 70% of flagged contradictions validated"* — no
   `pytest`, high value. *"Debate raised non-expert human accuracy from 60% to 88%"* —
   the check is a human adjudicator, at which point *external check* has stretched to
   cover "a person reads it," and a rule that covers everything constrains nothing.
   Literature synthesis, drafting, translation and summarisation are all high-value
   loops with no verifier.
3. **"Equals" is doing work the evidence cannot pay for.** Nothing here establishes an
   equality, or a monotone relation, or even that the relation is causal rather than
   selected — benchmarks get built where verification is cheap.

**The honest form is one word weaker and loses nothing:** *the reliability of an agentic
loop is bounded by the quality of the external check it closes on; where no check exists,
value is bounded by human review, which does not scale.* That still yields every
commitment in §8, still explains the 79.2 / 4.6 gradient once difficulty is
acknowledged as a co-cause, still puts *build the verifier first* at the top of the
list — and it survives PaperQA2, which the current form does not.

**Verdict: load-bearing, not decorative — and currently overstated by exactly one
verb and one table column.** Restore `K2`'s *Weakly* and *rubric graded*, add one
sentence conceding the difficulty confound, change *equals* to *is bounded by*. The
argument gets stronger, because a rule that has met its counter-examples in print is
worth more than one that has not.

## WHAT IS ACTUALLY GOOD

Short, and I would defend every line.

  recommendation engine, twenty years early, with a design rule that costs nothing.
  New, correct, and load-bearing.

  compressible resources, publish a number for each, publish the counter-anchor (FSI at
  1×), and publish the null that bites your own thesis (compressed format, worse grades,

  wince at.
